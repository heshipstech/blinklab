THE RULER FREEZES: baseline calibration after the validation round
==================================================================

Written 20 August 2026, before any code changed and before the corpus
re-run this document predicts. The owner decided the direction the
same day: robust passive learning first, guided calibration later if
still needed.

THE EVIDENCE THAT FORCED THIS
-----------------------------

The six-person round's pre-registered criterion 2 FAILED: three of six
volunteers' machines produced no usable baseline
(docs/validation-round.txt). The failures map exactly onto the two
halves of the current design:

1. The rise-only ratchet. After learning, the baseline may rise with
   a rolling window's p90, capped at 1.4 times that window's median,
   and never falls. P3's baseline rose 34.6 percent DURING its marked
   window; P4's rose 15.4 percent just before its window. Both
   sessions were excluded from the detector criterion because their
   ruler moved. And the ratchet is not only a live-session problem:
   measured on the published Eyeblink8 run, the baseline rose on
   EVERY one of the eight clips, by 2.3 to 37.6 percent, which means
   the published recall was earned partly by a blink line that grew
   more permissive as each clip went on.

2. The birth estimate. The baseline is born as the p90 of thirty
   seconds of samples, capped at 1.4 times the window median. The dry
   run's macbookair session was born at 1.41 times its session's
   resting median; the round's P3 and P5 landed at 1.33 and 1.28.
   The plan pre-registered 1.25 as the line past which a baseline is
   too long to be "open", and the learner's own ceiling sat looser
   than the plan's definition of implausible.

THE DESIGN
----------

Two changes, both to src/core/baseline.ts and its constants, nothing
else touched:

1. THE RULER FREEZES AT BIRTH. The rise ratchet is removed entirely.
   Thirty seconds of learning define what "open" means for this
   person on this camera, and the definition then holds for the whole
   session, exactly like any instrument that is calibrated and then
   used. Never-fall is preserved trivially, so a drooping lid still
   cannot lower the bar that would expose it, which was fix #126's
   non-negotiable. Drift becomes structurally zero: the failure that
   sank criterion 2 cannot recur, rather than being measured and
   excluded after the fact.

2. THE BIRTH CEILING TIGHTENS FROM 1.4 TO 1.25 TIMES THE LEARNING
   WINDOW'S MEDIAN, the plan's own pre-registered implausibility
   line. The learner should not be allowed to produce a baseline the
   round's soundness check would refuse on sight. The p90 itself
   stays: it exists so blinks during learning cannot drag "open"
   down, and that reason survives.

WHAT THIS FIXES, AND WHAT IT CANNOT
-----------------------------------

Fixed: every drift-class failure (P3, P4), by construction. Narrowed:
born-too-long, because the cap now matches the plan's line.

Not fully fixable at birth, stated plainly: the cap is measured
against the LEARNING window's median, and a learning window that
itself reads high (macbookair's read 7.51 against a whole-session
median of 6.93) still produces a high ruler no birth-time statistic
can detect, because the future is not available at calibration time.
The analysis-side soundness check remains the net for that case.

Traded away, stated plainly: the ratchet was also the only recovery
path for a ruler born too SHORT (a squint through the learning
window). A short ruler now stays short. No session of the twelve
measured in August was born short, the failure was always long, and a
short ruler is visible analysis-side as a baseline BELOW the session
median (over_resting under 1.0). A check for that belongs in the next
round's rules; it is recorded there rather than patched here.

THE PREDICTIONS, committed before the corpus re-run
---------------------------------------------------

The blink line is half the baseline, so freezing the ruler holds the
line at its birth level for a whole clip, where the published run's
line rose with the ratchet on all eight clips. A lower line late in a
clip demands deeper dips, so:

1. Total detections FALL from 430. The fall concentrates in the six
   clips whose ratchet exceeded 10 percent.
2. Recall falls from 87.7 percent. Predicted landing: between 82 and
   86 percent.
3. Precision (83.3 percent) HOLDS OR RISES: a permissive late-clip
   line is where shallow false alarms live, so invented detections
   should fall at least in proportion to real ones. If precision
   falls alongside recall, that reading is wrong and the record
   will say so.
4. The two clips whose ratchet was small (2.3 and 8.3 percent) move
   little.
5. The 1.25 birth cap binds rarely on this corpus: every clip's birth
   ratio measured 0.93 to 1.22 against its whole-clip median, so the
   corpus delta is dominated by the freeze, not the cap.

If recall lands BELOW 82 percent, the freeze costs more than this
design expected, and the result is published anyway and the trade
reconsidered in the open.

Whatever the numbers do, they are republished with this document,
the previous three runs stay in the record beside them, and the live
round's six sessions are NOT re-scored: they were measured by the
instrument that existed when they ran, and the published round table
stays exactly as published.

OUTCOMES
--------

Run 20 August 2026, eight clips, zero failures, evaluated against the
408 human-marked blinks. Four predictions held, one was wrong, and
the wrong one taught the most.

1. HELD. Total detections fell, 430 to 419, and every lost real
   detection sits in the six clips whose old ratchet exceeded 10
   percent. The two small-ratchet clips: one unchanged entirely, one
   unchanged on real blinks.
2. HELD. Recall landed at 83.6 percent, inside the predicted 82 to
   86, above the 82 that would have forced a reconsideration.
3. WRONG. Precision fell, 83.3 to 81.4: invented detections ROSE
   from 72 to 78. The prediction reasoned that a permissive late-clip
   line is where shallow false alarms live. Diffing the runs found
   the real mechanism: at the frozen, LOWER line, near-line flutter
   fragments into multiple crossings 200 to 400 ms apart, the same
   re-crossing signature P1 produced live in the validation round.
   Five of the six new false alarms sit in one clip's flutter
   episode, and adjacent pairs among them are textbook re-crossings.
   The freeze and the re-arm gate are therefore complementary: the
   freeze exposes the fragmenting the moving ruler used to paper
   over, and the gate is aimed at exactly that signature.
4. HELD. The small-drift clips moved little, see 1.
5. HELD, exactly. Every clip's birth baseline is IDENTICAL to the
   previous run's, so the 1.25 cap never bound on this corpus and
   the whole delta is the freeze. The freeze itself verified from
   the run's own records: baseline drift 0.0 percent on all eight
   clips.

Published in docs/eyeblink8-result.txt as the fourth number in the
record, with the third kept beside it. The round's six live sessions
are NOT re-scored; the published round table stands as measured by
the instrument that existed when they ran.
