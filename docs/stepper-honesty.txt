Stepper honesty on variable-rate clips
=====================================

Pre-registered 6 September 2026, ladder item A1 of REMEDIATION-2026-09.md,
the September audit's one critical finding (G-Stepped-1 in
docs/audit/2026-09-06-appendix-all-findings.md). Committed BEFORE the
code that implements it and BEFORE any clip is re-measured. The outcome
section at the end is appended after the regression run; nothing above
it is edited afterwards.

The defect
----------

The stepper (src/io/videoStepper.ts) learns a clip's frame interval from
the first six frames it can land on and takes the SMALLEST gap as the
interval, on the reasoning that a probe can skip a frame and see two
intervals but can never see less than one. That reasoning holds for a
constant frame rate clip and fails for anything else: one short gap
among the first six frames sets a step shorter than the clip's true
period. Every later target that falls inside a frame already showing
produces no frame callback, so the landing is "inexact", the stepper
falls back to the SCHEDULE time (origin + index x step) and counts the
frame as new. The same decoded frame is measured again under an
invented timestamp.

Reproduced by two independent verifiers through the real stepper
against a fake decoder with an explicit frame-time list: a 200 frame
clip at 20 frames per second with one short gap reports 399 frames at
40.0 frames per second with stoppedEarly false. The 25 fps refusal
passes on a clip the instrument declares unmeasurable, and blink rate,
PERCLOS, long closures and the score are published for it. The
duplicate-visit check (checkStepping, #193) cannot see it, because the
invented timestamps are monotonic and the pipeline accepts every one.

The rule, fixed here before any data is read
--------------------------------------------

1. Calibration collects up to twelve frame times (was six), within the
   same probe budget, and hands them to a pure function in src/core.

2. Every calibration gap must be a whole multiple of the smallest gap,
   within CALIBRATION_QUANTUM_S = 0.0015 s. The tolerance is one
   millisecond of container timestamp rounding plus margin: a 29.97 fps
   clip with millisecond timestamps alternates 33 and 34 ms gaps and
   must calibrate; a gap that is not a multiple of the smallest is what
   a variable frame rate looks like, and the run is refused by name
   before the first frame is measured.

3. The step is the MEAN period, (last time - first time) divided by the
   sum of the multiples, not the smallest gap. On a clip whose
   timestamps are exact the two are the same number. On a
   millisecond-quantised clip the smallest gap under-reads the period
   by up to a millisecond, the schedule drifts by that much per frame,
   and about one target in ninety lands inside the frame already
   showing. That drift is the "+1" the published Eyeblink8 table
   carries on two clips (docs/eyeblink8-result.txt) with no
   explanation; this is the candidate explanation, and the regression
   run decides it.

4. An inexact landing is a first-class fact. The stepper counts them
   and returns the count in its summary. When more than
   INEXACT_LANDING_TOLERANCE = 0.02 of the frames sought landed
   inexactly, the run is REFUSED by name, not summarised. The fraction
   mirrors STEPPING_DUPLICATE_TOLERANCE (0.98) in
   src/core/frameClock.ts: a seek that lands on the frame already
   showing is possible but rare once calibration is right, so more than
   two in a hundred is the step being wrong, or a browser that does not
   report where it lands, and either way the frame has no time of its
   own. A frame without a time of its own is not a measurement.

5. Every stepped export (the per-second CSV, the blink log, the frame
   trace) carries three header rows: frame_interval_s, frames_sought,
   inexact_landings. A kept file says what it is.

6. A glitch of exactly half the period cannot be told from a skipped
   probe at calibration (40 ms gaps are whole multiples of a 20 ms
   glitch). Such a clip calibrates at the glitch and is then refused by
   rule 4, because about half its targets land inside frames already
   showing. Both routes end in refusal by name; neither ends in a
   fabricated rate.

Predictions for the Eyeblink8 regression run
--------------------------------------------

The run is the owner's (ladder item D10, roadmap row 10.8): the eight
prepared clips, WebKit, tools/measure_corpus.mjs, on the machine that
produced the anchor run. Two branches are pre-committed; the
frame_interval_s row of each clip's export says which branch that clip
is in.

Branch A, a clip whose calibration gaps are all equal (exact
timestamps): the step is unchanged, the schedule is unchanged, the
export is byte-identical to the anchor run's apart from the three new
header rows, and inexact_landings is 0.

Branch B, a clip whose gaps alternate by one timestamp quantum: the
step changes from the smallest gap to the mean period. Predicted:
inexact_landings falls from roughly 1 in 90 to 0, frames_measured
falls by that same fabricated excess and matches the container's frame
count exactly (the coverage check's "+1" disappears), and the blink
match count on that clip moves by at most the number of blinks whose
annotated window sat within one frame of a fabricated timestamp, which
is at most a handful and in no consistent direction.

Across all eight clips: inexact_landings is 0 on every clip, no clip is
refused, and the published counts (341 of 408 caught, 65 invented)
stay within the anchor run's own repeatability, which the record
already states as digit-for-digit on two WebKit machines. A clip that
lands in neither branch, or a refusal on any corpus clip, falsifies
the rule as stated and is reported as such.

What would falsify the design, stated now
-----------------------------------------

- A corpus clip refused as variable rate: the quantum tolerance is too
  tight for real container timestamps, and the number and the gaps are
  reported before any tolerance is changed.
- inexact_landings above 0 on a branch A clip: the schedule drifts for
  a reason other than quantisation, and the run is not re-anchored
  until it is explained.
- A blink count moving on a branch A clip: impossible by construction
  (the schedule is unchanged), so it would mean the run is not
  repeatable and the anchor itself is in question.

Outcome
-------

(appended after the regression run; nothing above this line changes)

8 September 2026, the regression run at
68690bff63cbc3d0d2d7d754f85e219a3c28a4d5 (docs/eyeblink8-result.txt):

CONFIRMED: all eight clips measured stepped, none refused, and
inexact_landings is 0 on every clip. The design's own falsifiable
check passed everywhere.

REFUTED, rule 3's candidate explanation for the "+1": the coverage
block came back IDENTICAL to the anchor's, including measured 15784
against annotated 15783 and measured 5405 against 5404. An honest
stepper — mean period, whole-multiple gaps, zero inexact landings —
measured the same frame counts the old stepper did, so the "+1" was
never a fabricated frame from a drifting schedule. It is a property
of the clip and annotation pair. The branch A/B classification of
each clip (exact against quantised timestamps) awaits a read of the
frame_interval_s header rows, and gets a dated line here when read.

NOT AS PREDICTED, and not the stepper: "the published counts stay
within the anchor run's own repeatability" did not hold — 309 of 408
found and 62 invented, against 341 and 65. But every lost blink is on
one clip, 27122013_154548_cam, whose frames_measured did not move by
one, and the other seven clips reproduced the anchor digit for digit.
A stepper effect changes which frames are measured; these frames did
not change. The falsifier list above said a blink count moving on a
branch A clip is "impossible by construction... so it would mean the
run is not repeatable" — that construction silently assumed the
detector was otherwise the code the anchor measured, and it was not:
the caveat ledger in docs/eyeblink8-result.txt lists every detector
change between the anchors. The collapse is recorded there, and its
attribution — the per-miss replay of that clip's 37 misses — is the
measurement in progress.

9 September 2026, the attribution landed, and the count clause is
RESTORED. The collapse was a transient of the face tracker, not of
the stepper or any other change: five re-measurements at the same
commit — four of the clip alone, one of the full corpus in the same
shape — all reproduce the anchor, and the 9 September full run
returns 341 of 408 and 65 invented DIGIT FOR DIGIT
(docs/eyeblink8-result.txt, the transient section). So "the
published counts stay within the anchor run's own repeatability"
HELD for the reproducible run; the 8 September deviation was one
face-tracking failure in six runs, and it belongs to the model, not
the schedule. The frame_interval_s read also landed: all eight clips
calibrated to 0.033333 s and sought exactly the frames they
measured, so no clip took branch B's changed schedule, and the
branch A prediction — nothing moves — is what the reproducible runs
show.
