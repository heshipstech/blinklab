## 1. The honest state of the instrument today

1. **Blink detection on recorded clips is real and near its ceiling:** Eyeblink8 recall 83.6% (341 of 408; Wilson 95% 79.7–86.9), precision 84.0% (65 invented), F1 83.8%, reproduced digit-for-digit on a second WebKit machine; ~49 of the 67 misses are flat on both landmark signals, so the reachable headroom on the mesh alone is about 5 blinks — but the number is bound to a code commit and to no committed input, the evaluator that produces it has 0 tests, and the frame-stepper that feeds it fabricates frames on a variable-rate clip (a 200-frame 20 fps file reports 399 frames at 40 fps and sails through the 25 fps refusal).
2. **Blink detection on live cameras is worse and the instrument cannot see why:** the dry run lost 3 of 10 deliberate blinks on an iPhone at 30.7 fps and 1 of 10 on a MacBook at 29.2 fps with the 25 fps gate never firing, because the gate, the blink rate, the baseline sample floor and the velocity column all read processing ticks (24.4–46.0 fps across six volunteers on 30 fps cameras) rather than delivered photographs; peak closing velocity therefore scales with display-refresh over camera-delivery (up to 4× on a 120 Hz Mac), the learned ruler failed on 3 of 6 volunteers, and blink duration reads 96 ms on iPhones against 149–166 ms on Macs with no surviving explanation.
3. **Closures and the score are one-face constants without hysteresis:** the shut line is 0.4 of baseline derived from the owner's own floor (33%) and droop (45–50%); one 6 s closure fires 3 and 2 long-closure events on two iPhones (15–45 score points), the guided blink line sits 30–50% above the passive line so "blink duration" carries two definitions in one column, and a stored guided line follows the browser onto clips and strangers' faces with no trace in any export.
4. **Drowsiness evidence is modest, pre-registered and interval-free:** UTA-RLDD 148 videos / 54 subjects gives three-class balanced accuracy 0.498 (chance 0.333; 95% interval about 0.42–0.57) and alert-vs-drowsy 0.732, p 0.001; the demo score separates alert from drowsy at AUC 0.70 on 95 videos; DROZY is a null on 20 of 36 sessions; not one of these carries a confidence interval, the 30 RLDD exclusions rest on the stepper's six-frame calibration with no container cross-check, and MODEL_CARD still says the score was tested against "nothing external".
5. **Gaze is unmeasured and the honesty machinery is strong where it was forced and porous elsewhere:** "reliable near the centre" has no number behind it, the calibration solver accepts any fit, blinks are not excluded from fixations, and the ±25° pose gate is ~10× too loose for gaze; meanwhile 962 unit tests, 27 e2e tests, 376 pytest tests, 21 guard tools and a 98/95/100/98 coverage floor on `src/core` coexist with six refusal constants that survive mutation (`MIN_BLINK_FPS` bends to 23 or 28 green), no automated test that ever sees a face, `main.ts` wiring outside every floor, and ~16,400 lines of living prose (STATE.md 3,432 lines, LEARNING.md 309 KB) in which every contradiction found lives: 62% of file touches over the last 60 commits were prose.

## 2. The three biggest levers on accuracy and precision reachable in a browser

### Lever 1 — An observation is a delivered photograph, not a processing tick

**Evidence that it is a lever.** `processFrame` runs on every `requestAnimationFrame` tick (src/main.ts:4590-4599) and pushes a stability sample per tick (:3904-3911); `blinkShape.ts:84-96` divides by the tick gap; `measurableAtFps(fps)` at :3559 reads the tick rate; `blinkRate.ts:41-51` divides by elapsed time; `baseline.ts:74-77` and the guided phases count ticks. Consequences already in the record: velocity and A/V scale with display/delivery ratio (F-001) so the score's sluggish-lid penalty (150–300 ms) essentially never fires on a 120 Hz Mac; volunteers P2 and P6 processed at 45.2 and 46.0 fps on 30 fps cameras (1.5× inflation of every velocity they exported); a 15–24 fps dim-room camera behind a 60 Hz display passes the gate, exports blink counts at full confidence, and the participant report then asserts they were withheld (F-003); any face-loss gap dilutes the rate toward the calm end (F-004); a frozen ruler can be born from 25 distinct photographs (F-051); the per-tick core cost is quadratic in tick rate (5 ms at 120 Hz with a still gaze, F-044), which is thermal load on exactly the phones the goal names. The stepped corpus path is already frame-driven and is unaffected, so the ratchet is satisfied by construction.

**What moves.** Every live velocity/A/V value becomes device-comparable; the 25 fps refusal fires on the quantity the pre-registered rule names; the blink rate stops reading 0/min after face loss; inference drops 2–4× on fast machines and ~45% on phones. This is one data-model change (stamp every per-frame sample with `deliveredCount` and an observed-time accumulator; drive the camera path from `requestVideoFrameCallback` as the clip path already is; keep rAF for repaint and as fallback), not five patches.

**Harness that proves it.** (a) Before code: pre-register on exports already on disk that dry-run A/V scales inversely with processing/delivery ratio across the 30 / 55 / 126.7 fps machines, and that the same mechanism is a candidate for the 96 vs 149–166 ms duration gap (MediaPipe VIDEO-mode smoothing sees 4 near-duplicate stamps per delivered frame on a Mac and 1 on a phone; docs/blink-sample-rate.txt:472-481 names the stepped-vs-watched experiment). (b) Driver test: detect exactly once per presented frame. (c) Stepped Eyeblink8 rerun reproduces 341/408 and 65 digit-for-digit. (d) One A/B live session with a velocity prediction, not an equivalence expectation. (e) A verdict/report fixture with `sampled_fps` 20 and processing 60 whose measured rows and verdict agree.

### Lever 2 — One personal ruler, measured, verified, and carried with every number

**Evidence that it is a lever.** The validation round's one failed criterion was the ruler (baseline unsound on P3, P4, P5). The guided line is the midpoint (open+closed)/2 (guidedCalibration.ts:111) while the passive line is 0.5 × p90 (baseline.ts:107-111); with the instrument's own measured shut floor at ~33% of baseline that puts the guided line 30–50% higher as a fraction of open, so guided durations reach the score's slow-blink ramp 1.3–1.5× longer than priced, and for the one droop case the guided line was built for it makes droop-reads-closed worse (F-005). The closed median the procedure already measures is discarded; PERCLOS and long closures still use the owner's 0.4 (F-030). The long-closure reducer has no hysteresis while its sibling does (F-009), a single untrusted frame splits a closure and can hand its tail to the blink counter (F-036), and the calibration's own 3 s hold is scored as a microsleep and fed to the passive baseline (F-040). The open phase samples from the frame the 26-word instruction appears (G-Guided b-1), there is no soundness ceiling (a line at 0.85 of open passes every check, G-Guided b-4), no verification blink (G-Guided b-5), and no export says which line or what mm produced a session's numbers (F-007, G-Guided b-6) — so round II's frozen plan cannot read a guided session.

**What moves.** The two heaviest score inputs and the strongest RLDD features become person-referenced rather than owner-referenced; "blink duration" gets one definition; 10.9's device gap becomes an arithmetic check on existing `shutBaselineMm`/`apertureMm` columns; phone long-closure counts collapse from 3 and 2 to 1; the pilot's exported numbers stop carrying an instructed closure.

**Harness that proves it.** (a) Trace harness prediction before the hysteresis change: phone shapes 3→1 and 2→1, desktop unchanged, durations byte-identical; a pinned test that a 6 s closure with ±0.3 mm noise counts once. (b) The owner's recorded droop session through both line placements, published. (c) Guided placement re-derived as closed + k × (open − closed) with k pre-registered from the 10.7a noise floor (median wobble 0.020 mm, p95 0.286 mm), and a settle window derived from the same doc. (d) A 5-second verification phase after resolve — "blink three times" — storing the line only if ≥2 of 3 are caught, logged as an outcome per calibrated person with n_open, n_closed and separation ratio; that log IS the first measurement of the procedure, and it needs no corpus. (e) Export columns `blinkLineMm`, `blinkLineSource`, `shutLineMm`, `shutLineSource` appended trailing so older headers stay a prefix; round II reads them.

### Lever 3 — More photographs per blink: negotiate 60 fps first, then add the appearance witness

**Evidence that it is a lever.** `getUserMedia` has never been asked for a frame rate (camera.ts:28-32: width/height ideals plus deviceId, nothing else). The committed catch tables (docs/blink-sample-rate.txt) show shallow quick blinks are LOST at 30 fps — rate is a floor, not a count — and the dry run lost 30% of deliberate blinks on the phone. Independent of tick inflation, the single finite difference reads 81–88% of true peak velocity at 25–30 fps against 96–97% at 60, with A/V inflated 1.12–1.37× (F-033, reproduced). Amendment 16 already names 13.2 as the era's "real lever" and then schedules it ~26 rows behind new-signal work (F-025). Past the sampling loss sits the mesh ceiling: 49 of 67 misses are flat on both landmark signals, and the blendshape head is trained on closure, unlike the mesh; it is the only browser-feasible route past that ceiling (12.2/12.3), and it is scheduled after the sibling corpora are spent, which would force a second read (F-072).

**What moves.** If any owned camera negotiates ≥55 fps, the marginal band closes and every live claim of the era stops inheriting the 30 fps catch loss; if none does, that is the era's ceiling and it should be stated before 18 signals are built on it. Either outcome is a result the record does not yet hold.

**Harness that proves it.** (a) One constraint plus `getCapabilities().frameRate.max` on every owned device, recorded in a dated docs section with declared max, negotiated rate and any resolution change — half a day. (b) One deliberate dim-room session so F-003's pre-registered refusal has an observation. (c) 13.3's two pre-committed branches (improved catch; empty set). (d) The ground truth for any live catch claim must be a cued schedule rendered on the existing `lightSchedule` overlay pattern (per-event caught / missed / latency at zero privacy cost, F-071), because the count-ten-between-marks protocol is already shown contaminated (3 of 6 pressed Mark more than twice). (e) For the witness: 12.3's neutrality check (the same 67 misses reproduce digit-for-digit with the head enabled) and a per-miss verdict CSV, run on the regression harness before any sibling corpus is opened.

## 3. What the roadmap gets right, what it gets wrong, and what to change

### Right — protect these

- The era rules: instrument before claim, prediction before read, split at the core/io fence, one more UTA-RLDD read and never a per-feature drip. None of the reorders below touches a published number without a run.
- Eyeblink8 as a regression harness only, with the detector ratchet and the "Built from commit" anchor.
- Refusal-not-default in every reducer and every loader, the frozen-or-refused baseline, the frame trace, and the five-column table that keeps wrong answers published.
- Capability as a property of a setup (13.6), the microsleep vocabulary cap, and the pupil result kept dormant on its own evidence.
- The generated-and-guarded documentation layer (results block, cannotSee, model provenance, stamps): nothing in it was found wrong.

### Wrong

- The ordering serves the labelled read (12.18) and the ledger, not the live instrument the goal names: 13.2 and 13.8 sit behind 26 rows that never touch the capture chain, and 13.8 is filed as performance when it corrupts an exported column.
- Gaze signal rows (12.11–12.13, the 12.18 riders) precede the gaze instrument (14.9), inverting the era's own rule.
- No row fixes long-closure hysteresis, the evidence-rate gate, line provenance, stepper honesty on variable-rate clips, an engine baseline, a cued ground-truth protocol, or the missing "ended" session state — the defects with the largest effect on numbers people will see.
- 11.8's three-week n-of-1 would run on a page that redeploys 19–20 times on a busy day; 12.18's v1-parity gate would reject a corrected stepper as a regression.
- The "42 µs core chain" sentence is false at 120 Hz (5 ms, quadratic fixation scan), and "one checkbox is one branch" describes a shape the work no longer has (PRs #374–#403 share one branch).
- 10.3 is framed as a coin flip; it should be decided: retire the three DROZY shape rows formally, because the ~3 h re-measure would re-run a null on a sample missing its sleepiest sessions, on a rate the stepper has not verified, to defend "suggestive" verdicts that pass by chance at p 0.5.

## 5. Risks to the maintainer and how to keep the pace sustainable

- **The volume is the risk, not the code.** 283 commits in 30 days and 63 in the last 7 by one person; 208 of the last 335 file touches were prose; every finding about a false sentence lives in the hand-synchronised layer, and the guarded layer has zero. The next audit will find the same class with new numbers unless the surface shrinks. Rule: any number that appears in more than one document is generated or appears once with links; the PR body is the canonical narrative; STATE.md carries a generated STATUS block plus one line per PR; REMEDIATION.md and NEEDS-REVIEW.md are closed at their first line; docs/log.md is retired in writing; docs/audit is archived. Target: living prose halves by the end of Phase 11 and no new prose file without a guard that reads it.
- **Ritual cost per row.** A row today rewrites four to five prose files. Cut it to two: the PR body and the one doc the row's Check names. Write the ADR only for intent or measurement-definition changes (ADR-0005 for the era priority and the PROJECT.md contradiction; the 13.1 mobile ADR) — the record shows four such changes with no ADR and dozens of prose files touched instead.
- **Measurement time.** Eyeblink8 is ~20 minutes per run, DROZY ~3 hours, RLDD a re-download. Do not spend the 3 hours on 10.3; retire the rows. Spend corpus minutes only behind a committed prediction (already the rule) and only after increment 2 makes the stepper refuse what it cannot measure.
- **Solo reviewer.** Every guard is armed by its own sibling test and the deploy publishes four minutes before CI reports; make Pages wait for CI (`workflow_run`, with `head_sha` passed to checkout and `GITHUB_SHA`), and add the guardsArmed test, so a tired Friday merge cannot ship a red page.
