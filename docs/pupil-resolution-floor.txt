HOW MANY IRIS PIXELS DOES THE PUPIL ESTIMATOR NEED?
Roadmap 13.11. Prediction committed before the sweep was written or run.

WHY THE QUESTION EXISTS
  The light-response experiment (9.4, docs/pupil-light-result.txt) resolved
  the pupil in 1 of 239 in-window seconds, 0.4 percent, against a 50 percent
  gate. Face and pose tracking were flawless throughout, which localised the
  failure to the eye region rather than to the face model. The result page
  named the small webcam iris, a 43 px median, as the suspected limit, and
  named eyeglass reflections as a likely aggravator.

  "Suspected" is the operative word. Nobody has measured what the estimator
  needs. Row 13.12 stays dormant until optics that clear this floor become
  accessible, and it cannot be armed against a floor nobody has established.

THE PREDICTION, one line as the row asks:
  The model-derived floor will land BELOW 43 px of iris width, and I expect
  it between 12 and 30 px at a blur of about one pixel.

WHY I EXPECT THAT, so the reasoning can be wrong in public
  The estimator casts 16 rays from the iris centre and walks each in 40
  steps to the iris radius, sampling luminance bilinearly, so the crossing
  it reports is located to about one fortieth of the iris radius: at 43 px
  that is a step of roughly half a pixel, which is finer than the pixel
  grid. Sampling resolution is therefore not what should bind.

  What should bind is CONTRAST after blur. The estimator refuses below a
  centre-to-rim contrast of 0.12, and blur flattens exactly that. A pupil
  at a physiologically ordinary 0.4 of the iris diameter has a radius of
  about 0.2 of the iris width, so at 43 px the pupil radius is around 9 px.
  A Gaussian blur of one pixel leaves a 9 px feature substantially intact.
  For the contrast gate to bite, the pupil radius has to fall to roughly
  two or three times the blur, which at a one pixel blur means an iris of
  something like 15 to 30 px.

WHAT IT WOULD MEAN EITHER WAY
  If the floor lands below 43 px, then iris pixel count ALONE does not
  explain the 0.4 percent yield, and the real-world failure has another
  cause: reflections, exposure, motion, the eyelid, or the estimator
  meeting a real eye rather than a synthetic disc. That would make the
  result page's "43 px webcam iris" line an incomplete explanation, and
  correcting it would be this row's most useful output.

  If the floor lands at or above 43 px, the suspicion is confirmed, 13.12's
  arming condition gets its number, and the reasoning above is wrong in a
  way worth understanding.

WHAT THIS NUMBER IS NOT
  Model-derived, on synthetic fields, until real optics clear it. A
  synthetic dark disc on a bright annulus is not an eye: it has no lashes,
  no lid, no specular highlight, no sensor noise and no motion. The floor
  measured here is a LOWER BOUND on what a real eye needs, and the gap
  between it and reality is the whole reason 13.12 waits for optics rather
  than for arithmetic.
