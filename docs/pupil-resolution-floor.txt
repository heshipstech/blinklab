HOW MANY IRIS PIXELS DOES THE PUPIL ESTIMATOR NEED?
Roadmap 13.11. Prediction committed before the sweep was written or run.

WHY THE QUESTION EXISTS
  The light-response experiment (9.4, docs/pupil-light-result.txt) resolved
  the pupil in 1 of 239 in-window seconds, 0.4 percent, against a 50 percent
  gate. Face and pose tracking were flawless throughout, which localised the
  failure to the eye region rather than to the face model. The result page
  named the small webcam iris, a 43 px median, as the suspected limit, and
  named eyeglass reflections as a likely aggravator.

  "Suspected" is the operative word. Nobody has measured what the estimator
  needs. Row 13.12 stays dormant until optics that clear this floor become
  accessible, and it cannot be armed against a floor nobody has established.

THE PREDICTION, one line as the row asks:
  The model-derived floor will land BELOW 43 px of iris width, and I expect
  it between 12 and 30 px at a blur of about one pixel.

WHY I EXPECT THAT, so the reasoning can be wrong in public
  The estimator casts 16 rays from the iris centre and walks each in 40
  steps to the iris radius, sampling luminance bilinearly, so the crossing
  it reports is located to about one fortieth of the iris radius: at 43 px
  that is a step of roughly half a pixel, which is finer than the pixel
  grid. Sampling resolution is therefore not what should bind.

  What should bind is CONTRAST after blur. The estimator refuses below a
  centre-to-rim contrast of 0.12, and blur flattens exactly that. A pupil
  at a physiologically ordinary 0.4 of the iris diameter has a radius of
  about 0.2 of the iris width, so at 43 px the pupil radius is around 9 px.
  A Gaussian blur of one pixel leaves a 9 px feature substantially intact.
  For the contrast gate to bite, the pupil radius has to fall to roughly
  two or three times the blur, which at a one pixel blur means an iris of
  something like 15 to 30 px.

WHAT IT WOULD MEAN EITHER WAY
  If the floor lands below 43 px, then iris pixel count ALONE does not
  explain the 0.4 percent yield, and the real-world failure has another
  cause: reflections, exposure, motion, the eyelid, or the estimator
  meeting a real eye rather than a synthetic disc. That would make the
  result page's "43 px webcam iris" line an incomplete explanation, and
  correcting it would be this row's most useful output.

  If the floor lands at or above 43 px, the suspicion is confirmed, 13.12's
  arming condition gets its number, and the reasoning above is wrong in a
  way worth understanding.

WHAT THIS NUMBER IS NOT
  Model-derived, on synthetic fields, until real optics clear it. A
  synthetic dark disc on a bright annulus is not an eye: it has no lashes,
  no lid, no specular highlight, no sensor noise and no motion. The floor
  measured here is a LOWER BOUND on what a real eye needs, and the gap
  between it and reality is the whole reason 13.12 waits for optics rather
  than for arithmetic.

SCORED, 7 September 2026. The sweep was written and run after the
prediction above was committed and pushed.

THE TABLE
  docs/evidence/2026-09-07-pupil-floor/resolution-sweep.csv, one row per
  iris width and one column per blur, 1 where the estimator resolved a
  pupil and 0 where it refused. Regenerated and compared digit for digit
  by test/core/pupilFloor.test.ts, so the file cannot drift from the
  generator that made it.

THE FLOOR, the smallest iris width that resolves at each blur:

  blur 0.0 px    4 px      blur 2.0 px     8 px
  blur 0.5 px    4 px      blur 2.5 px    12 px
  blur 1.0 px    5 px      blur 3.0 px    13 px
  blur 1.5 px    8 px      blur 4.0 px    18 px

THE PREDICTION, scored honestly in both halves

  DIRECTION: correct, and not marginally. The prediction said the floor
  would land below 43 px. At every blur tested it does, and at the
  softest blur in the sweep it is 18 px, less than half the iris the
  light-response run actually had. A 43 px iris resolves at every blur
  this sweep covers.

  MAGNITUDE: wrong. The prediction expected 12 to 30 px at a blur of
  about one pixel. The measured floor at one pixel is 5 px, well under
  the low end of that band. The reasoning that produced it — that the
  contrast gate bites once the pupil radius falls to two or three times
  the blur — over-estimated how much contrast the estimator needs. At
  a 5 px iris the pupil radius is 1 px against a 1 px blur, and it
  still clears the 0.12 gate. The band was too cautious by a factor of
  two to six, and it is left standing above rather than corrected.

WHAT THIS MEANS, which is the useful part

  Iris pixel count alone does NOT explain the 0.4 percent yield of the
  light-response run. The owner's camera delivered a 43 px median iris,
  and 43 px is above the floor at every blur measured here, by a wide
  margin. So the sentence in docs/pupil-light-result.txt that localises
  the limit to the small webcam iris is INCOMPLETE. Something else did
  most of the work of that failure: the candidates the plan already
  named are eyeglass reflections and exposure, and the candidates this
  sweep cannot speak to are lashes, the lid, motion and sensor noise,
  none of which a synthetic disc has.

  Row 13.12 stays dormant. Its arming condition now has a number, but
  the number says the arming condition was never really about pixels.

A NON-MONOTONE TAIL, recorded rather than trimmed
  Below 4 px of iris the sweep is not monotone: 2 px resolves, 3 px does
  not, 4 px does. At 3 px the pupil is 1.2 px across, so there is no
  disc to find and the answer turns on where the raster's centre lands.
  That is the geometry talking and not the estimator. The sweep declares
  4 px as the smallest meaningful width, the monotonicity test runs
  above it, and a separate test pins the 2/3/4 flip so the minimum
  cannot be quietly raised to hide a real island later.

  The first version of this sweep started at 8 px and reported a floor
  of 8 px at most blurs, which was the edge of the grid rather than the
  floor. It was extended downward before anything was written here.

WHAT THIS NUMBER IS NOT, restated because it matters more after a result
  Model-derived. A dark disc on a bright annulus is not an eye. The
  floors above are a LOWER BOUND on what a real eye needs, and the
  distance between them and reality is exactly the gap the 0.4 percent
  yield is sitting in.
