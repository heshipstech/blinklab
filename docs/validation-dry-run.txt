The validation round's DRY RUN, 16 August 2026
==============================================

Four sessions, one face, the owner's, on three devices within about an
hour. This is NOT the six-person round. It is the rehearsal that had to
happen before six strangers were asked, and its purpose was to find what
fixtures cannot. It did.

The raw CSVs are NOT in this repository. They sit in
$DATASETS/validation-dry-run. Reproduce this table with:

    cd analysis
    PYTHONPATH="$PWD" .venv/bin/python tools/validation_report.py \
        "$DATASETS/validation-dry-run"

P1 and P2 are the SAME device and the same protocol, run twice. P1's
ten deliberate blinks are not between its marks, and the owner did not
remember whether the sequence had slipped, so it was run again as P2
rather than guessed at. P1 is kept because deleting a session that
disagrees with you is how a result stops meaning anything.

What this found, in order of how much it matters
================================================

1. THE MEASUREMENT TRAVELS BETWEEN DEVICES. WHAT IS LEARNED FROM IT
   DOES NOT.

   The same face gave a median eyelid aperture of 6.88, 7.05, 6.93 and
   6.33 mm on the four sessions, within 11 percent of each other. The
   iris normalisation works: it is doing exactly the job it was built
   for, cancelling distance and resolution.

   The baselines learned from those measurements were 7.69, 8.09, 9.80
   and 7.30 mm, a spread of 34 percent.

   On the MacBook Air a handful of frames in the first 30 seconds read
   up to 10.35 mm against a window median of 7.51. The baseline is a
   90th percentile, so it followed them and settled at 9.80, which is
   1.41 times that session's own median aperture. Its blink line landed
   at 71 percent of resting against 56, 57 and 58 on the other three,
   so a blink had only to close 29 percent of the way to be counted.
   That session logged 26.0 blinks per minute, and one of its
   detections has an amplitude of 1.26 mm, below the 1.5 mm line the
   on-screen table greys out as too faint to be a blink.

   The baseline check as pre-registered could not see any of this.
   Readiness passed on all four. Drift passed on all four, INCLUDING
   the session with the wrong ruler, because a baseline born wrong does
   not move. Its drift is 0.0. The plan's second dated correction adds
   `baseline_over_resting`, flagged above 1.25.

2. THE IPHONE MISSED 3 OF 10 DELIBERATE BLINKS, AND MISSED THEM IN
   RUNS RATHER THAN AT RANDOM.

   P2 followed the protocol exactly. Seven blinks were detected between
   the two marks. Their spacing:

       mark 1 at 42.77 s
         blink 45.66   gap 2.89 s
         blink 46.63   gap 0.97 s
         blink 47.63   gap 1.00 s
         blink 48.63   gap 1.00 s
         blink 51.60   gap 2.98 s
         blink 52.50   gap 0.90 s
         blink 53.47   gap 0.96 s
       mark 2 at 53.95   gap 0.48 s

   A metronome cadence of 0.90 to 1.00 seconds, interrupted by two holes
   of 2.89 and 2.98 seconds. Each hole is the width of two more blinks
   at that cadence. The person did not pause; the instrument stopped
   reporting.

3. BLINK DURATION DEPENDS ON THE RATE THE PAGE HAPPENS TO RUN AT.

   Same face, same day. Median detected blink duration and how many
   frames that spans at each session's own processing rate:

       iPhone,  31.7 fps    68 ms     2.2 frames
       iPhone,  30.7 fps    96 ms     2.9 frames
       MacBook, 32.4 fps   166 ms     5.4 frames
       Sony,   126.7 fps   129 ms    16.4 frames

   Read the iPhone against the Sony, because those two are the pair
   whose blink lines agree (57 and 58 percent of resting). The Sony
   samples a blink sixteen times and calls it 129 ms. The iPhone samples
   it three times and calls it 96 ms, a quarter shorter. The MacBook's
   166 ms is not comparable: a lower bar keeps the aperture below it for
   longer, so its durations are inflated by the same defect as its
   count.

4. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE 25 FPS FLOOR, STATED CAREFULLY.

   Remediation D1 was written around a slow CAMERA holding the gate
   open, because the page reports its own animation rate. That is real
   and P4 shows its size: the Sony session displayed 126.7 frames per
   second while the camera declares 30, an overstatement of four times.

   This dry run raises a larger question that D1 as written does not
   cover. Both sessions with a CORRECT blink line and a processing rate
   near 30 missed deliberate blinks. The only session near 30 that
   caught all ten did so with a ruler 41 percent too long. The only
   session with both a correct ruler and a high rate caught all ten.

   So the suspicion is that 25 frames per second is not enough for this
   detector, and the floor is set too low rather than merely reported
   wrongly.

   THIS IS A LEAD AND NOT A RESULT. One session per device, one face,
   one day. Device, processing rate, lighting and camera all move
   together across these four files, and nothing here separates them.
   The next test that would is a second MacBook session at the same
   ~32 fps with a baseline that lands correctly: if that also misses
   about three of ten, the rate is the cause and the device is not.

5. ONE ANOMALY THAT DID NOT REPEAT.

   P1 counted a single continuous six second closure as three long
   closures, one per second. P2, P3 and P4 each counted their closure
   as one. Recorded because it happened, not diagnosed, and one
   occurrence in four is not enough to call it a defect.

6. THE TOOL ITSELF NEEDED NO FIXES.

   Eight real files, no refusals, and the pairing coped with device
   names inserted into the filenames. That was not a foregone
   conclusion, and it is the one thing this rehearsal was expected to
   break.
