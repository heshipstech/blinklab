THE PROBE THAT CANNOT SEE A FAST CLIP
Roadmap 10.14c. Written 7 September 2026, BEFORE the code, as this
repository requires of anything it measures.


THE MECHANISM

Calibration finds the clip's frame interval by seeking forward in
small steps and recording where it actually lands. The step is
CALIBRATION_STEP_S in src/io/videoStepper.ts, and it is 10 ms.

Ten milliseconds is one whole frame period at 100 frames per second
and more than one at 120. A probe that advances by a whole period
lands on every other frame, so every gap calibration observes is two
periods rather than one.

The rule that followed 10.14 asks that every gap be a whole multiple
of the smallest. Gaps that are uniformly two periods satisfy it
perfectly: the smallest gap IS two periods, every gap is one times the
smallest, and the mean period those multiples imply is two periods.
Calibration therefore returns half the true rate, and because two
periods is a whole number of frames, every scheduled target lands
exactly on a real frame. The inexact-landing count stays at zero, so
10.14's refusal, which exists to catch a schedule the browser could
not honour, cannot see this at all. The run reports a confident wrong
rate and measures half the clip.

The comment beside the constant still says "4 ms is under half a frame
even at 120 frames per second". That was true of 4 ms. The constant
was raised to 10 ms and the sentence was left behind, which is how the
claim survived being false.


WHAT IS PREDICTED, AND HOW IT IS SCORED

P1. On a synthetic clip whose frame times are k/120 seconds, the
    CURRENT stepper reports a frame rate at or below 60 and measures
    at most half the frames, with ZERO inexact landings. If the
    inexact-landing count is not zero, the mechanism above is wrong
    and this row is rewritten before any fix is written.

P2. After the change, the same fixture measures every frame and
    reports 120.

P3. The 60 frames per second fixture the end-to-end suite already
    uses measures the same count as it does today, within one frame.

P4. A synthetic 30 frames per second clip measures the same count and
    reports the same rate before and after the change.

P5. Eyeblink8's published recall and precision are unchanged by this
    row. Its clips are 30 frames per second, so P4 is the reason to
    expect it; only the owner's regression run (roadmap 10.8a) can
    score it, and it now scores TWO stepper changes rather than one,
    this row and ladder A1's whole-multiple rule.


THE DECISION RULE, FIXED NOW

If P3 or P4 fails, the change is REVERTED rather than shipped with a
caveat. A fix for fast clips that moves the behaviour of ordinary ones
is not this row, and the whole point of writing P3 and P4 down before
measuring is that discovering them afterwards is not a discovery.

If P1 fails, no fix is written until the mechanism is understood,
because a fix aimed at a mechanism that is not there would be a change
to the instrument justified by nothing.


THE SHAPE OF THE FIX, PRE-REGISTERED

The probe step stops being a constant. Calibration takes its first two
landings at the current step, and from then on uses a quarter of the
smallest gap it has seen, floored at 2 ms so a pathological clip
cannot drive it to zero. A quarter of a period cannot step over a
frame at any rate, which is the property the stale comment claimed and
the constant had stopped providing. The comment goes with it.

This is pre-registered as a voiding condition for roadmap 11.4: a
TalkingFace run measured before this change, on a clip faster than 100
frames per second, would have been measured at half its rate.


SCORED, 7 September 2026, against the fake-video harness in
test/io/videoStepper.test.ts, which drives the real stepThroughVideo
over a clip with an explicit frame-time list.

P1  FAILED. The prediction said a clip at 120 frames per second would
    be measured at half its rate. It is measured exactly right, and it
    was before this change. The mechanism above is wrong about WHERE it
    bites. A 10 ms probe on a 120 fps clip produces a MIXTURE of one
    and two period gaps, and ladder A1's whole-multiple rule recovers
    the true period from a mixture exactly. The premise needs every gap
    to come out the same, which needs the step to be a whole multiple
    of the period.

    Recorded rather than corrected, because that is what a prediction
    is for. The row was written in amendment 17 against a stepper that
    no longer exists: A1's rule landed in between and had already
    absorbed most of this.

WHAT WAS ACTUALLY MEASURED, sweeping the rate with clip length held at
one second and again at three, to rule length out:

    100 fps   measured correctly, before and after
    120 fps   measured correctly, before and after
    150 fps   measured correctly, before and after
    200 fps   measured correctly, before and after
    240 fps   ZERO frames before, correct after
    300 fps   ZERO frames before, correct after

    The boundary is between 200 and 240, at both clip lengths, so it is
    the rate and not the length.

    The failure is a zero, not a wrong number. By 240 the constant
    probe is more than two frame periods and calibration cannot gather
    enough distinct landings, so it returns no interval and the run
    measures nothing. The instrument did not publish a confident wrong
    rate for a fast clip; it visibly failed to measure one. That is a
    better failure than the row assumed and still a failure: a phone's
    slow-motion recording could not be stepped at all.

P2  Held, in the amended form. Every rate from 100 to 300 measures
    every frame with zero inexact landings.

P3  Held. The 60 frames per second case measures 60, unchanged.

P4  Held. The 30 frames per second case measures the same count and
    calibrates to the same interval before and after.

P5  Not scorable here. Eyeblink8's clips are 30 frames per second and
    P4 held, which is the reason to expect no movement, but only the
    owner's regression run (roadmap 10.8a) can score it, and it now
    scores two stepper changes rather than one.

THE DEVIATION FROM THE PRE-REGISTERED FIX, stated because it is a
deviation. The shape above said a quarter of the smallest gap, floored
at 2 ms, and that is what shipped. A half was also measured: it fixes
every rate too and costs about half the probe attempts. It was NOT
taken, because half of a gap that is itself two periods is exactly one
period, which is the pathology being escaped, and at 250 frames per
second the first gaps come out as two and three periods. A quarter is
under one period even when the estimate it is taken from is four times
too large. The attempt cost at 30 frames per second rises from 38 to
45 of a 60 budget, which is the price and is recorded here rather than
discovered later.
