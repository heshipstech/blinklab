THE PROBE BUDGET AT ORDINARY RATES
Roadmap 10.14d. Written 7 September 2026, BEFORE the measurement, as
this repository requires of anything it measures.


WHAT 10.14c LEFT BEHIND

Calibration may spend CALIBRATION_ATTEMPTS seeks, which is 60, to
gather CALIBRATION_FRAMES distinct frames, which is 12. Row 10.14c
replaced a constant 10 ms probe with a quarter of the smallest gap
seen so far, floored at 2 ms, because a constant probe could not
measure a clip above 200 frames per second at all.

A quarter of a period is safe and slow. Three of every four probes
land on the frame already showing, cost an attempt and teach nothing.
That row measured the price at one rate: 30 frames per second went
from 38 attempts to 45 of the 60 available. It recorded the number and
handed the question here rather than pretending the budget was fine.

The budget has to cover more than the arithmetic. Every imprecise
landing, one where the browser answered `seeked` but no frame callback
arrived, also costs an attempt and adds no frame. On a real clip in a
real browser those are not rare. So the question is not only how many
attempts the rule needs on a perfect clip; it is how much room is left
over for the clip that does not answer cleanly.


WHAT IS PREDICTED, AND HOW IT IS SCORED

All predictions are about the fake-video harness in
test/io/videoStepper.test.ts, which records every seek the stepper
asks for, on a clip whose frame times are exact. There are no
imprecise landings in that harness, so what it measures is the
arithmetic floor: the fewest attempts the rule can possibly spend.

P1. Attempts to gather 12 frames are roughly FLAT across rate, not
    rising with it. The rule is proportional to the period it just
    measured, so 30, 60, 120 and 240 frames per second should all cost
    about the same number of probes, within 5 of each other.

P2. That flat cost is between 40 and 50 of the 60 budget at every rate
    from 30 to 300.

P3. The margin left for imprecise landings is therefore under 20
    attempts, or under a third of the budget, at every rate.

P4. Raising the step from a quarter to a half of the smallest gap
    would cost about half as many attempts, and would still measure
    every rate from 30 to 300 correctly ONCE the smallest gap is a
    true single period. It is the early gaps, before that is true,
    where a half is unsafe.

P5. The 60 frames per second end-to-end fixture and the 30 frames per
    second case measure the same count and the same interval after
    whatever change this row makes as before it.


THE DECISION RULE, FIXED NOW

If P1 fails, and attempts rise with rate, then the rule is not
proportional in the way it was designed to be and this row rewrites
the rule rather than the budget.

If P2 and P3 hold, the margin is too thin and something changes. The
row does NOT get to pick the change after seeing the numbers: the
order of preference is fixed here, cheapest and least behavioural
first.

  1. Adapt the step. Once the whole-multiple rule can be run over the
     landings gathered so far and returns a period, use half of THAT
     period rather than a quarter of the smallest raw gap. The
     quarter exists to survive a smallest gap that is up to four
     times the true period; a reduced period is not that, so the
     safety it buys is already bought.
  2. Raise the budget. A seek is cheap in the harness and not free in
     a browser, so this is second, and if it is taken the new number
     is justified by the measured margin rather than rounded up.
  3. Change the floor. Last, because 2 ms is what stops a pathological
     clip driving the step to zero and it is not what costs attempts
     at ordinary rates.

If P5 fails at any point, the change is REVERTED rather than shipped
with a caveat, on the same grounds as 10.14c: a fix for the budget
that moves the behaviour of ordinary clips is not this row.

If P4 fails, option 1 above is off the table and the row takes option
2, because a rule that measures a rate wrongly is not a saving.


WHAT WILL BE COMMITTED EITHER WAY

A table of attempts against rate, measured, in this file. Not a
sentence saying the budget is comfortable. The margin left for
imprecise landings stated as a number of attempts, so a later row that
makes landings less reliable can see what it is spending.


SCORED, 7 September 2026, against the fake-video harness in
test/io/videoStepper.test.ts, which records every seek the stepper
asks for. Clips have exact frame times and the harness always answers,
so what is measured is the ARITHMETIC FLOOR: the fewest probes the
rule can possibly take.

P1  FAILED. Attempts are not flat across rate and they do not rise
    with it. They FALL, from 51 at 24 frames per second to 20 at 300,
    a spread of 31 where the prediction allowed 5. The reason is that
    the quarter is taken from the smallest RAW gap, and at slow rates
    that gap already is one period, so the step is a quarter of a
    period and four probes are spent per frame. At fast rates the 2 ms
    floor binds instead and the step is a larger share of the period.

P2  FAILED. 40 to 50 held from 24 to 120 but not above: 150, 200, 240
    and 300 came in at 35, 28, 23 and 20.

P3  FAILED as a universal claim, and held where it matters. The margin
    is 9 attempts at 24 frames per second and 40 at 300.

THE FINDING THE PREDICTIONS DID NOT ANTICIPATE, and the reason this
row is worth more than a tuning note. At 20 frames per second and
below, the budget was EXHAUSTED: calibration spent all sixty attempts
and gathered eleven frames at 20, eight at 15, then calibrated on
those. CALIBRATION_FRAMES is twelve rather than the original six
because one short gap among six frames set the whole schedule, which
is the September audit's critical finding. That protection was
thinning out at exactly the rates where the reproduced defect lived: a
20 frames per second clip reported as 40.

THE DECISION RULE DID NOT COVER WHAT WAS MEASURED, and that is stated
rather than papered over. It was written for attempts RISING with rate
and for P2 and P3 both holding. Neither happened. So the ordered
preference was applied to the facts as found, and each step below is
recorded with what it was measured to do.

  1. THE RULE ADAPTS, taken. Once the landings reduce under the
     whole-multiple rule, the step is half of THAT period rather than
     a quarter of the smallest raw gap; the quarter stands for
     landings that do not reduce, where there is no period to halve
     and inventing one would be the mistake this module refuses.
     Measured: 30 frames per second falls from 48 attempts to 41, 60
     from 44 to 25, 120 from 42 to 23, 300 from 20 to 19. Nothing
     below 30 moves, because the step is capped at the bootstrap and
     at slow rates the cap is what binds.

  2. LIFTING THAT CAP, measured and REJECTED. Allowing the reduced
     half past the bootstrap step brings 24 frames per second from 51
     attempts to 30 and 30 from 41 to 28, which is the whole slow-end
     problem solved. It also switches off a refusal. On a 201 frame
     clip carrying one frame 25 ms out of place, the larger probe
     strides over the anomaly, calibration never lands on it, the
     whole-multiple rule sees a clean 40 ms period, and the run
     reports 200 frames at a confident 25 frames per second with zero
     inexact landings, where before it refused the clip by name as
     variable rate. A cheaper calibration that cannot see a bad clip
     is not a saving. The cap stays, and the reason it stays is now
     written where it is, because it was not the reason the cap was
     given when it was added.

  3. THE BUDGET RISES, taken, to 120 from 60. Justified by the floor
     rather than rounded up: 81 attempts at 15 frames per second, 61
     at 20, 51 at 24. Sixty could not gather twelve frames at 20 or
     below. A hundred and twenty covers 15 with 39 spare, and at 24 —
     the slowest rate this project will measure blinks on, since
     MIN_BLINK_FPS is 24 — leaves 69 attempts for probes a browser
     answers without a frame callback.

  4. THE FLOOR, untouched. PROBE_STEP_FLOOR_S is what stops a
     pathological clip driving the step to zero, and it is not what
     costs attempts at ordinary rates.

P4  Held, in a narrower form than it was written. A half of a REDUCED
    period measures every rate from 15 to 300 correctly and costs
    about half what the quarter cost at 60 frames per second and
    above. It cannot be had at slow rates without lifting the cap,
    which item 2 measured and rejected, so the saving is real above 30
    and absent below it.

P5  Held, and more strongly than it was written. Every rate in both
    tables above calibrates to the SAME interval before and after, to
    the last digit a double carries: 0.03333333333333333 at 30 frames
    per second either way, 0.016666666666666666 at 60. Only the number
    of probes spent reaching that answer changed. This is also the
    argument that Eyeblink8's published numbers cannot move: its clips
    are 30 frames per second, the calibrated interval there is
    identical, and the schedule the run walks is derived from that
    interval and the origin alone, so an identical interval means an
    identical schedule means identical frames measured.

THE STATED MARGIN, which is what this row owes the next one. At every
rate this project will measure blinks on, calibration's arithmetic
floor leaves more attempts spare than it spends. The tightest is 24
frames per second at 51 of 120. Below the blink floor, 15 frames per
second spends 81 and keeps 39. A row that makes landings less reliable
— a browser that answers fewer probes, a slower grace, a clip that
seeks badly — is spending out of that margin, and
summary.calibrationAttempts is where it shows.

THE PRICE, recorded rather than discovered later. A clip that cannot
calibrate now spends up to a hundred and twenty seeks before refusing
rather than sixty. Each seek that lands inexactly waits out
FRAME_GRACE_MS, so the worst case before a refusal roughly doubles.
That is paid only by clips that were going to be refused anyway.
