INFERENCE ONCE PER PHOTOGRAPH: THE ARITHMETIC, PRE-REGISTERED
=============================================================

Written 10 September 2026, before any dry-run number is read for
this question, in the predict-then-verify discipline. Roadmap row
13.8a, brief A2. This row ships NO CODE: it is the arithmetic that
row 13.8b's driver change either survives or does not, scored on
exports that already exist.

WHAT THE MECHANISM IS
---------------------
The camera path runs `processFrame` on every animation tick, and an
animation tick is the DISPLAY's event, not the camera's. Past the
camera's delivery rate the loop re-reads the same photograph: the
24 August correction to the sample-rate warning measured an M5 Max
processing 120 frames per second on a 30 fps camera reading 30
distinct photographs and 90 duplicates. Every dry-run session
declared a 30.0 fps camera, and the processing rates ranged from
29.2 to 126.7 — so the re-read ratio r = processing / 30 ranged
from about 1 to about 4.2.

Peak closing velocity is where the re-reads stop being harmless.
`analyzeClosing` (src/core/blinkShape.ts) takes the maximum over
ADJACENT sample pairs of aperture drop divided by timestamp gap,
and the samples are one per processing tick. When the same
photograph is read r times, the whole frame-to-frame drop lands
between the LAST duplicate of one photograph and the FIRST read of
the next — the same millimetres over one PROCESSING interval
instead of one delivery interval. The drop is unchanged; the gap is
divided by r; the velocity is multiplied by about r. The September
audit reproduced this with the repository's own module: a 30 fps
step read at 30, 60 and 120 Hz reads 120, 240 and 480 mm/s — the
same blink, three velocities, linear in r.

Amplitude reads the same photographs and does not care how often:
it is a difference of two apertures, not a rate. So the
amplitude-over-velocity ratio A/V — the duration-like quantity the
drowsiness literature watches — is DEFLATED by about r.

One interference is named before the numbers are: MediaPipe runs in
VIDEO mode, whose temporal smoothing sees r near-duplicate
timestamps per photograph on a fast machine. If smoothing spreads
the frame-to-frame drop across several ticks, the inflation is
damped below r. The synthetic reproduction above bypassed MediaPipe
entirely, so it establishes the arithmetic, not the field size.

WHAT WILL BE COMPUTED
---------------------
From the retained dry-run exports in $DATASETS/validation-dry-run
(the blink logs, blinklab-blinks-*.csv, which have carried
amplitudeMm and peakClosingVelocityMmPerS since the log existed):
per session, over every logged blink, the MEDIAN amplitude, the
median peak closing velocity, and the median of per-blink A/V
computed as amplitude / velocity × 1000 — computed from the two
base columns rather than trusted from the third, with the blink
count n reported beside every median. Sound sessions only, the same
four the dry-run doc uses for its rate claims:

    session         processing fps   re-read ratio r
    macbookair2         29.2             ~1.0  (control)
    iphone2             30.7             ~1.0  (control)
    iphone17promax      55.0             ~1.8
    pcsony             126.7             ~4.2

THE PREDICTIONS
---------------
P1, the clean pair. iphone2 and iphone17promax are the same device
family, same face, and their median blink durations already agree
to a millisecond (96 vs 97 ms), so the device confound is minimal.
If the inflation is real and undamped, the iPhone 17's median
velocity reads about 1.8 times the iPhone 14's, and its median A/V
about 0.55 times — for the same physical blinking.

P2, the ordering. Across the four sound sessions, median A/V orders
INVERSELY with r: pcsony lowest, iphone17promax next, the two ~1.0
controls highest. Median velocity orders WITH r, pcsony highest.

P3, the magnitude. pcsony at r ~4.2 reads a median velocity at
least twice macbookair2's. Device differences cannot make that
number: the same face's median duration differs between those two
sessions by 15 percent (129 vs 149 ms), not by a factor of two.

THE FALSIFIER, NAMED BEFORE THE NUMBERS
---------------------------------------
If the iPhone pair's A/V ratio comes back near 1 (above 0.8), or
the P2 ordering fails, the inflation is damped or absent on the
LIVE path and VIDEO-mode smoothing is the named suspect. That
outcome would NOT cancel 13.8b — the driver change is a correctness
row either way, because a timestamp that means "the display
repainted" is the wrong denominator for a camera measurement even
when smoothing hides most of the damage — but the benefit claim
would have to be restated from "velocities are inflated up to 4x on
fast machines" to whatever the damping measured, and this section
is where that restatement would be recorded.

Whichever way it lands, the outcome section below is written from
the computed table, dated, and 13.8b does not open before it
exists.
