THE LEARNING WINDOW, DECIDED, AND A PREDICTION
==============================================

Written 9 September 2026, before the change is made and before any
new measurement is read, in the predict-then-verify discipline.
Roadmap row 10.13b; the decision was taken the same day by the
owner's delegation and is recorded in ROADMAP amendment 23.

WHAT IS DECIDED
---------------
For roughly the first 30 seconds of every session — live or clip —
the passive baseline is still learning and the personal blink line
does not exist yet. Through that window the detector counts blinks
against the boot-time fixture line of 4 mm, a constant from one
face. This behaviour predates the column that revealed it: since
roadmap 10.13a every per-second export carries blinkLineSource, and
the 10.8a corpus run's exports read `fixed` for the first ~29 rows
of every one of the eight clips, then `passive` to the end
(docs/line-in-the-export.txt, the 9 September re-score). The
behaviour was never a decision anybody wrote down. Now it is.

The decision: KEEP the fixture line for the learning window, and
LABEL the condition everywhere the count travels. Three options were
weighed:

  Feed null while learning. Discards blinks the fixture line
  demonstrably catches — the anchor's found blinks include the
  pre-freeze window on every clip — and blanks the demo's first
  half minute. Rejected for throwing away true detections to
  satisfy a purity rule.

  Count on the live learning line. A line that moves while it is
  being learned is the moving ruler this project already rejected
  once, when it froze the baseline (docs/baseline-freeze.txt).
  Rejected for the same reason it was rejected then.

  Keep the fixture line and say so. The count stands, and the
  CONDITION becomes visible: the record states that early-window
  blinks are measured against a one-face constant rather than the
  person's own line, and the per-row blinkLineSource column already
  says it frame by frame. Chosen.

WHAT THE CHANGE WILL BE, AND WHAT IT WILL NOT TOUCH
---------------------------------------------------
Labels only. The page will say, in its session surface, that the
first ~30 seconds are counted against the fixed 4 mm line;
docs/eyeblink8-result.txt will record the condition beside the
numbers it applies to; MODEL_CARD's conditions will state it; and a
test will hold the statements in place, the same shape as every
other pinned sentence in this repository.

No counting behaviour changes. Not the fixture constant, not the
baseline freeze, not the line selection, not a threshold, not a
timer. The diff will contain no change to any DETECTOR_SOURCES file
watched by tools/detectorRatchet.mjs, and the ratchet stays green
without a caveat — a checkable property of the increment, not a
promise.

THE PREDICTION
--------------
Scored against the 10.8a anchor (docs/eyeblink8-result.txt, the
current block at 68690bff63cbc3d0d2d7d754f85e219a3c28a4d5) on the
owner's next corpus run at a commit carrying this change:

  The delta is ZERO. All eight clips: the same found, miss and
  false counts, digit for digit — 341 of 408 found, 65 invented,
  recall 83.6%, precision 84.0%, F1 83.8%. No clip moves in either
  direction, because a label is not a measurement.

  The line-source distribution is unchanged: blinkLineSource reads
  `fixed` for the first ~29 rows of every clip and `passive` after,
  exactly as the 9 September exports read.

One caution inherited from the record: a re-measure that disagrees
on ONE clip gets a re-run before it is believed. The 8 September
transient (docs/eyeblink8-result.txt, the transient section) showed
the face tracker can lose a face once and ride the loss to the end
of a clip, unreproducibly; a single-clip disagreement is checked
against that mechanism before it is read as this change's delta.

WHAT WOULD REFUTE THIS
----------------------
  If any committed Eyeblink8 number moves REPRODUCIBLY on a run
  whose only detector-window change is this increment, then
  something labelled here also touched behaviour, the "labels only"
  claim is false, and the diff — not the corpus — is where the
  defect lives.

  If the next run's blinkLineSource distribution differs from the 9
  September read (fixed for ~29 rows, then passive), the premise
  this decision stands on was a property of those runs rather than
  of the code, and the decision is re-opened with the new read in
  front of it.

This prediction is not edited after the fact; the outcome becomes a
new dated section below it.
