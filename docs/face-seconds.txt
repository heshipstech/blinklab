SAMPLE FLOORS COUNT FACE-SECONDS, NOT TICKS, AND A PREDICTION
=============================================================

Written 9 September 2026, before the change is made, in the
predict-then-verify discipline. Roadmap row 10.12c, brief A20.

WHAT THE DEFECT IS
------------------
The baseline floor demands 100 samples and each guided calibration
phase demands 30, and a sample is a TICK: one pass of the processing
loop that was fed an aperture. The loop runs at the display's pace,
not the camera's, and past the camera's rate it re-reads the same
photograph — the 24 August correction to the sample-rate warning
established that an M5 Max processing 120 frames per second on a
30 fps camera reads 30 distinct photographs and 90 duplicates. So on
a 120 Hz display, 100 ticks can span 0.83 seconds of actual face
time, and a guided phase's 30 ticks a quarter of a second: floors
meant to demand a second of held evidence are satisfied by
duplicates of a handful of photographs.

WHAT THE CHANGE WOULD BE
------------------------
Both floors additionally require a minimum span of TRUSTED-FACE
TIME, counted the way roadmap 10.12b's rate counts observation: each
fed frame credits the gap to the previous fed frame, capped at one
frame interval at the slowest measurable rate (1000 /
MIN_BLINK_FPS = 40 ms). The floors are DERIVED from the existing
ones rather than chosen: the baseline's 100 samples at the slowest
legal rate is 4000 ms of face time, and a guided phase's 30 samples
is 1200 ms. The tick floors stay — the change only ADDS a
condition, so nothing that fails today can start passing.

Face-seconds become visible: shown on the page while the floors are
filling, and exported per row as a trailing column, so a session
whose floors were starved of face time says so in its own record.

THE PREDICTION
--------------
On the stepped corpus, byte-identical output. Three reasons, each
checkable: a clip is stepped frame by frame, so every tick is a
DISTINCT photograph and deliveredCount does not exist there; the
clips run near 30 frames per second, so inter-frame gaps of ~33 ms
sit under the 40 ms credit cap and face-seconds accumulate at the
pace of elapsed face time; and every one of the eight clips birthed
its baseline on the anchor run, so each learning window held a face
for far longer than 4000 ms. A floor that is satisfied everywhere
the old floor was satisfied changes no output.

Face-seconds equal elapsed seconds on every corpus clip whose face
is never lost inside the window measured. The corpus-wide record
allows few exceptions: the awake autopsy counted exactly 4
no-trusted-face miss spans across all eight clips
(docs/miss-character.txt), so at most a handful of seconds can
differ anywhere. The per-clip traces live on the owner's machine,
so WHICH clips differ is named when the next run computes the
column, and the scoring of this prediction includes that naming.

A 120 Hz display no longer satisfies the floor on 25 photographs:
25 distinct photographs credit at most 25 x 40 = 1000 ms, under the
guided phase's 1200 ms, however many duplicate ticks the display
adds — the row's own synthetic, and a test verbatim. The baseline
floor likewise stops accepting 100 duplicate-fed ticks that span
less than 4000 ms of face time.

WHAT WOULD REFUTE THIS
----------------------
  If any committed Eyeblink8 number, any exported corpus byte, or
  any baseline birth certificate moves on the next run, the "only
  adds a condition" claim is false and the added floor is binding
  somewhere this document argued it could not.

  If a clip's face-seconds differ from its elapsed seconds by more
  than its recorded no-trusted-face spans explain, the credit rule
  is dropping time it witnessed or crediting time it did not.

  If the 25-photograph synthetic passes either floor, the credit
  cap is not doing what the sample-rate record says it must.

This prediction is not edited after the fact; the outcome becomes a
new dated section below it.
