THE IRIS RULER, CHECKED AGAINST A KNOWN LENGTH
==============================================

Written 13 September 2026, before any scale bar is measured. Roadmap
10.7d, the scale-bar half of ladder B12. Every millimetre this page
publishes comes from one conversion: the visible iris is taken as a
ruler 11.7 mm wide, and any length in pixels becomes a length in
millimetres by

    apertureMm = openingPx * (IRIS_DIAMETER_MM / irisWidthPx)

(src/core/aperture.ts, IRIS_DIAMETER_MM = 11.7 at src/core/constants.ts).
That conversion has been checked once for its CONSTANT — the 11.7 mm
value carries its citation and a ±4% population spread (roadmap
10.10c4d; MODEL_CARD's measurement-uncertainty section, and the
constant's own comment) — and never for its VALIDITY as a ruler: does a
known physical length read back correctly through it, and does the
reading hold as the person moves nearer and farther? A printed bar of
known width is the first external check on the ruler itself, the way
Eyeblink8 was the first external check on the blink detector.

WHY THE POPULATION SPREAD DOES NOT ANSWER THIS. The ±4% is one number,
the between-person variation of the real iris, and it is a BETWEEN-
person error: it says a stranger's aperture figures may sit 4% high or
low because their iris is not exactly 11.7 mm. It says nothing about
whether the ruler is internally sound for ONE setup — whether a length
the ruler did not choose reads back at its true size, and whether that
holds across working distance. Those are separate questions, and a page
whose every millimetre rests on this one conversion should have both
answered and recorded side by side, so neither is mistaken for the
other.

THE MEASUREMENT, the owner's step, on the owner's physical setup. A
scale bar of known width printed on paper — a length W_true chosen so
it spans a clear fraction of the frame — is held flat and facing the
camera at THREE working distances: near, typical and far for this
setup. At each distance the app's iris ruler reads the bar's apparent
width W_measured, from the same frames, with the same person's iris as
the ruler. Three distances, because a ruler that is right at one and
wrong at another is a distance-sensitive ruler, and only more than one
distance can tell the two apart.

THE RULE THE MEASUREMENT PATH MUST KEEP. Absolute-millimetre
thresholds stay OUT of the path that reads the bar. W_measured is the
raw ruler conversion above — pixels times 11.7 over the iris width in
pixels — and it must not pass through any absolute-mm gate: not
BLINK_APERTURE_THRESHOLD_MM, not the shut line, not any refusal stated
in millimetres. A scale bar is not a blink, and a reading filtered
through a blink threshold would be testing the threshold as much as the
ruler. The check measures the instrument's ruler, nothing downstream of
it.

THE PREDICTION, committed before the bar is measured:

    W_measured is within ±5% of W_true at ALL THREE distances.

WHY ±5%, AND WHY IT IS A CHOICE NOT A DERIVATION. The ±4% population
spread does not apply here and is not the bar: this is one setup and
one iris measured against a length of KNOWN size, so the between-person
variation cancels — the same iris that may sit at the population edge
rules both the bar and every aperture beside it, and its offset divides
out of a same-setup comparison. What ±5% bounds instead is the
DISTANCE-and-geometry error of the ruler for this setup: whether the
bar and the iris sit close enough to the same depth plane that they
scale together as the person moves, plus the printing, positioning and
pixel-reading error of the physical check itself. ±5% is stated as a
bound the ruler should clear if the iris is a sound ruler, chosen
before the numbers rather than fitted to them.

WHAT EACH OUTCOME REDIRECTS.

  - Within ±5% at all three distances: the ruler is validated for this
    setup, and the ±4% population spread remains the dominant and
    SEPARATE uncertainty. The two are recorded side by side so a reader
    of the spread sees the physical check next to it and does not read
    one as the other.
  - A drift beyond ±5% that GROWS with distance: the bar and the iris
    are not on the same depth plane, or foreshortening and falling
    resolution move the iris width faster than the bar — the ruler is
    distance-sensitive, and the lever is the working-distance proxy
    (14.9a's pause past a tenth of the iris width), not the constant.
  - A roughly CONSTANT offset at all three distances: the bar's printed
    width or this person's iris departs from 11.7 mm by that factor,
    which is exactly what the ±4% spread already allows and what
    cancels within one person — a note about this setup's iris, not a
    fault in the ruler.

WHERE THE RESULT WILL BE WRITTEN. A dated section appended below this
one, and the outcome carried beside the ±4% population spread in
MODEL_CARD's measurement-uncertainty section, so the physical check on
the ruler sits next to the population statement it complements. This
prediction is not edited after the bar is measured; the outcome becomes
a new dated section here.
