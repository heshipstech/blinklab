DEPTH-QUALIFIED CLOSURE EPISODES, AND A PREDICTION
==================================================

Written 17 September 2026, before the change is made, in the
predict-then-verify discipline. Issue #115, filed 6 August 2026 by
the amendment 5 adversarial review; carried since then as the known
limitation on record in MANUAL item 44. The current behaviours below
are MEASURED on today's detector over the traces this document
defines; the predictions are for the detector after the change.

WHAT THE DEFECT IS
------------------
Two detectors, two clocks, two boundaries. blink.ts times a closure
from the frame the aperture leaves the blink line. The long-closure
detector times from the frame it reaches the deeper shut line. Time
spent between the two lines counts toward nothing, so a slow drowsy
descent — the microsleep-onset shape 6.2 and 6.3 exist for — pays
its descent time as pure alert delay, and a closure whose truly-shut
span stays under half a second is missed entirely however long the
whole episode held. The verified consequence, executed against both
reducers with the owner's measured numbers: 300 ms drooping through
the band plus 400 ms fully shut, a genuinely shut 700 ms closure, is
refused by the blink counter (too long) and missed by the long
closure detector (truly shut span under the 500 ms line). It lands
in neither bin.

WHAT THE CHANGE WOULD BE
------------------------
Depth-qualified episodes, the redesign direction the issue itself
states:

  A closure EPISODE begins the frame the aperture leaves the blink
  line — the same episode boundary blink.ts uses, fed the identical
  per-frame line value (guided-aware), restoring a shared partition.
  The episode clock runs from that crossing, and the episode ends
  the frame the aperture is back at or above the blink line.

  The episode counts as a LONG CLOSURE only if it reached truly
  shut — strictly below the shut line — at some point. A steady
  droop that never reaches shut stays a non-event, whatever its
  length. The shut line itself does not move: 40 percent of the
  frozen baseline, the same number PERCLOS reads.

  The event fires at the earliest honest moment both conditions
  hold: the clock past the blink maximum AND shut reached. For a
  descent that reaches shut early, that is 500 ms after leaving the
  blink line; for a descent that dawdles in the band past 500 ms, it
  is the frame shut is first reached.

  The re-arm gate and the bounded untrusted gap keep their 10.11
  form, re-anchored to the episode boundary: re-arm is the blink
  line cleared by the same noise-floor fraction blink.ts re-arms
  with, and an over-bound gap abandons the episode together with its
  reached-shut memory, gate closed.

WHAT DOES NOT CHANGE
--------------------
blink.ts: untouched, not one line. The exports: longClosureCount is
the only key the detector feeds, and it keeps its name, type and
column; values change, keys do not. PERCLOS: untouched, still the
frozen shut baseline. Eyeblink8: the corpus scores blinks and no
DETECTOR_SOURCES file moves, so the published recall is untouched by
construction. The 12.0a shut-line placement pre-registration: its
instrument (analysis/blinklab/shutline.py) reads aperture columns
offline and never consults episode state, so every frozen clause
stands; a dated note beside it records that prediction P3's
live-event phrasing predates this redesign. DROZY: the published
caveat already names long closures as moved since the measuring
commit, and this change is one more reason it must stay until the
re-measure.

THE PARTITION, fix #126's constraint answered
---------------------------------------------
Fix #126 destroyed an earlier design because it could count one
closure as both a blink and a long closure. Here both reducers time
from the SAME crossing of the SAME line: blink.ts counts an armed
closure at or under 500 ms, this detector fires strictly beyond
500 ms and only if shut was reached. One closure, at most one bin.
The deliberate remainder: an episode past 500 ms that never reached
shut is nobody's event — that is the droop non-event, now defined by
depth rather than by which line the clock happened to start on.

CORRIDOR CAVEAT, stated not solved
----------------------------------
The qualification is only meaningful while the shut line sits below
the episode boundary. A guided blink line at or below the frozen
shut line inverts the corridor: every episode then qualifies on
entry and the detector degrades, honestly and pinned by a unit test,
to the single-line design. The cure — a personal shut floor learned
from observed blink minima — remains issue #113's and 12.0a's, not
this increment's.

PREDICTIONS
-----------
Baseline 7.2 mm where a baseline is named: blink line 3.6, shut
line 2.88, the owner's 2026-08-05/06 measured numbers.

P1  The 700 ms two-phase closure (300 ms at 3.4 mm, then 400 ms at
    2.35 mm). Today: count 0, no alert, no duration. After: count 1,
    fired at 500 ms plus at most one frame from the blink-line
    crossing, recorded duration within one frame of 700 ms.

P2  The reading droop (3.4 mm held, any length). Today: count 0.
    After: count 0, at three seconds and at any hold length.

P3  A full deliberate closure, both lines crossed within one frame
    step. After: count, fire time and recorded duration identical to
    today's within one frame.

P4  The 10.11 iPhone hover traces (centre 2.9 plus-minus 0.3 mm
    against shut 3.04, blink line 3.8). After: still exactly one
    event each, at 30 and at 120 Hz.

P5  Alert latency. Full closure: unchanged, 500 ms plus at most one
    frame. Slow descent reaching shut inside 500 ms: the alert
    arrives at 500 ms from the crossing, no longer 500 ms after
    reaching shut — the descent time stops being pure delay.

REFUTERS
--------
Any prediction above failing is a refutation, and so is either of:
a trace on which one closure lands in both bins, or the droop of P2
producing any event at any length. A refuted prediction is recorded
here in a dated section below, never edited away.
