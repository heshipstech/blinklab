WHAT THE FIXTURE'S ROUNDING IS WORTH, AND A PREDICTION
======================================================

Written 7 September 2026 (roadmap 10.10c4e, ladder B12, audit F-094),
before any number below is computed, in the predict-then-verify
discipline.

WHY IT EXISTS
-------------
docs/aperture-noise-floor.txt publishes this instrument's stillness
figure: the median absolute frame-to-frame change in aperture, 0.020
mm per eye, measured over the 300 committed frames of
test/fixtures/session-01.json. Every millimetre-scale judgement in this
project is read against that floor. The shallow-blink work talks in 0.4
mm bands; the validation round chased 0.1 mm disagreements.

That fixture does not store what the landmarker produced. It stores
each coordinate rounded to four decimal places of normalised image
coordinate, to keep the file to a fifth of its full-precision size
(src/core/fixtureRecording.ts). So part of the measured wobble is the
eye, and part of it is the grid the numbers were written onto, and the
published floor does not say how much is which.

If the grid is coarse relative to the floor, then the floor is partly a
measurement of the file format, and a reader comparing a 0.1 mm claim
against it deserves to know that.

THE METHOD, FIXED NOW
---------------------
Arithmetic over the committed fixture, using the real functions.

One rounding step is 1e-4 of a normalised coordinate. In pixels at the
fixture's own 1280 by 720 that is 0.128 horizontally and 0.072
vertically. The aperture is the MEAN of two near-vertical chords, so
moving one chord endpoint by one step moves the mean by about half a
step, and the millimetre conversion is that pixel amount times
IRIS_DIAMETER_MM over the fixture's own median iris width in pixels,
which is measured rather than assumed.

The iris width is measured with irisWidthPx, the same function the
instrument uses, over the same frames and index sets the noise floor
row used. No new pipeline, no new convention.

THE PREDICTION
--------------
Written before running it.

1. The vertical step is the one that matters, 0.072 px, because the
   chords the aperture averages are near vertical. The horizontal step
   is larger in pixels, 0.128, and irrelevant to the numerator.

2. The fixture's median iris width lands between 25 and 40 pixels: it
   is a webcam close-up at 1280 by 720, and an iris much outside that
   range would mean the frames are not what the noise floor row
   described.

3. One quantum of stored precision is therefore worth between 0.010
   and 0.017 mm of aperture, taking the half-step for the mean of two
   chords and converting with the measured iris width.

4. The published floor of 0.020 mm is therefore between about ONE and
   TWO quanta. That is the finding this row exists to state, and the
   consequence follows either way: the stillness floor is the same
   order as the grid the fixture is written on, so it bounds the
   instrument and the storage together and cannot separate them.

5. The audit's phrasing is "two rounding quanta". If the measurement
   lands nearer one than two, the audit's figure is recorded as
   approximate rather than adopted, the same as the yaw figure in the
   row before this one.

WHAT THIS ROW DOES NOT DO
-------------------------
It does not re-record the fixture at full precision. That is the
separate row the ladder names, and it needs a recording session rather
than arithmetic. This row states what the existing floor is worth so
that a reader is not misled by it in the meantime, and so that whoever
re-records knows what improvement to expect.

It also does not claim the floor is WRONG. A bound that includes the
storage grid is still an upper bound on the instrument's own wobble,
which is the direction that matters: the eye is at least as still as
the floor says, possibly stiller.

THE RESULT, MEASURED, 7 September 2026
======================================

Computed by the committed arithmetic (src/core/storageQuantum.ts) over
the fixture's 270 still frames, both eyes, and recomputed by
test/core/storageQuantum.test.ts on every run, so these numbers cannot
drift from the code:

  stored step, horizontal: 0.128 px    vertical: 0.072 px
  median lid-chord tilt from vertical: 13.2 degrees
  median iris width: 43.3 px (37.5 to 50.0 across kept frames)
  one quantum of aperture: 0.0097 mm

  left median frame-to-frame change:  0.0198 mm = 2.03 quanta
  right median frame-to-frame change: 0.0194 mm = 1.99 quanta

THE PREDICTION, SCORED
----------------------
Part 1 HELD. The lid chords run 13.2 degrees off vertical at the
median, and the horizontal grid would only take over past 29.4, which
is arctan of the two steps' ratio. So the finer vertical grid is what
the aperture rides, even though the horizontal grid is nearly twice as
coarse in pixels. This was the part most worth checking rather than
assuming, because it is the one the whole conversion rests on.

Part 2 WAS WRONG. The median iris width is 43.3 px, outside the
predicted 25 to 40. The band was reasoned from the framing — "a webcam
close-up at 1280 by 720" — when the file was sitting there to be
measured. The frames are a closer close-up than that phrase implies.

Part 3 WAS WRONG, by three percent, and for part 2's reason alone. One
quantum is worth 0.0097 mm, just under the predicted floor of 0.010.
The predicted band and the measurement are the same arithmetic with a
different ruler: 0.036 px buys fewer millimetres through a 43.3 px
iris than through the 40 px the band's floor assumed. Counting this as
a second independent miss would be counting one mistake twice.

Part 4 landed on the top edge. The published floor is TWO quanta, 2.03
on the left eye and 1.99 on the right. The prediction bracketed "about
one to two" and the answer turned out to have one value rather than a
range. The order was right, which is the finding the row exists for.

Part 5 does not trigger. It said that if the ratio landed nearer one
than two, the audit's phrase "two rounding quanta" would be recorded
as approximate rather than adopted. It landed on two, so the audit's
figure is adopted as measured.

WHAT IT MEANS, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
-----------------------------------
The trap here is worth stating plainly, because the ratio invites the
wrong conclusion. Two quanta of measured wobble does NOT mean the
floor is made of storage. Rounding is deterministic: a perfectly still
eye rounds to the same stored value every frame, and the measured
frame-to-frame change would be zero, not two quanta. The grid only
becomes visible once the true value is already moving across it.

What the ratio does establish is that the floor and the grid are the
same size. That is enough to settle the question this row asked: from
this fixture, no part of the 0.020 mm can be attributed to the
instrument rather than to the file, in either direction. The floor
remains an upper bound on the instrument's own wobble — the eye is at
least as still as it says, possibly stiller — and a reader comparing a
0.1 mm claim against it now knows the bound is ten times the claim's
resolution and cannot be sharpened without re-recording at full
precision.

That re-recording is the ladder's separate row. This row says what to
expect from it: the floor can fall by at most the grid's contribution,
and nothing here can say how much of it that is.
