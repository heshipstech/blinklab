THE CAPTURE-RATE CLAIM: DOES 60 CATCH WHAT 30 MISSES?
=====================================================

Written 13 September 2026, before any 30-and-60 session is run and
before 13.2's device day says whether any setup here can reach 60.
Roadmap 13.3. Both branches below are committed now so that the data
selects neither.

WHAT THIS SCORES, AND WHY IT IS NOT ALREADY SCORED. docs/blink-sample-
rate.txt established, deterministically and with no camera, that the
sampling rate decides whether a blink is counted inside a band about
0.4 mm wide: a firm blink is caught at every rate from 25 Hz up, a
too-shallow one never, and between them the rate governs. Its own
numbers, from the real detector over synthetic traces:

    a 3.30 mm-minimum blink   67% of phases at 30 Hz -> 100% at 60 Hz
    a 3.40 mm-minimum blink   48% at 30 Hz            -> 96% at 60 Hz
    an 80 ms blink at 3.20 mm 43% at 30 Hz            -> 87% at 60 Hz
    a firm blink (<= 2.80 mm) 100% at every rate, unchanged by 60

That file was careful to say what it could NOT establish: whether real
blinks live in the band often enough for the rate to matter in
practice. The reason is a permanent limit on the exports — THE DATA IS
CENSORED. A blink the detector misses writes no row, so every blink log
is drawn from the blinks that were caught, and the population that would
settle the question is exactly the one the instrument cannot record. No
re-reading of a free-behaviour session gets around it.

The cued protocol (11.0a/b) is what breaks the censoring, and it is why
this row exists. Each blink cue is a REQUESTED blink at a known instant,
so the denominator is the cues, not the detections: catch rate is
cues-answered over cues-issued, and a missed cue is a counted zero
rather than a silent absence. cue_events.py already scores a blink cue
against the blink log's first event in the response window. Running that
same protocol at 30 and at 60 frames per second, on a setup that can
reach 60, scores the sampling prediction against a denominator the dry
run never had.

A setup counts as reaching 60 only if it is 60-CAPABLE by the condition
13.2 committed before any device was read (docs/frame-rate-
negotiation.txt): a negotiated rate of at least 55 fps AND the iris
ruler holding at or above 22 pixels, so a camera that buys 60 by
dropping resolution below the ruler's floor does not qualify — that
would change the instrument, not just its rate.

BRANCH A — A 60-CAPABLE SETUP EXISTS. The prediction, committed before
the sessions:

  1. At 60 fps the cued catch rate is HIGHER than at 30 fps on the same
     setup and person. The gain is not uniform: it concentrates on
     SHALLOW and QUICK cued blinks, exactly the band above, and firm
     deliberate blinks are caught at both rates. A reader who splits the
     answered cues by the amplitude the caught ones report should see
     the 30-to-60 improvement sitting in the shallow tail.
  2. The SIZE is bounded by the sweep, not free: 60 fps catches at or
     near 100% of cued blinks whose depth reaches the band (3.40 mm and
     below), while 30 fps sits well under that, so the per-setup gain is
     on the order the sweep's band predicts (tens of percentage points
     where the person's cued blinks fall shallow, near zero where they
     do not). Each rate's catch rate is published with its Wilson
     interval (stats.wilson_interval), because a cued session is a
     handful of cues and a bare percentage would overstate it.
  3. THE DEVICE-INDEPENDENCE CONTROL (15.4a). The SAME recorded clip,
     stepped through the detector on each device, must catch its blinks
     identically to within one frame, because a stepped run sees every
     frame regardless of the live rate. A live 30-versus-60 difference
     that VANISHES under stepping is the sampling effect and nothing
     else; a difference that PERSISTS under stepping is a device-
     measurement artefact, not the rate, and names its own next
     instrument row rather than being read as sampling.

  THE FALSIFIER, fixed here: if the 60 fps catch rate does not exceed
  the 30 fps rate by more than their Wilson intervals overlap — that is,
  no separable improvement — then the sampling-band mechanism does not
  govern real cued blinks on this setup, whatever the synthetic sweep
  shows, and the next instrument row is the aperture-depth distribution
  of the cued blinks themselves (do real cued blinks reach the band at
  all), not a third rate. An improvement LARGER than the sweep's ceiling
  allows (60 fps beating 30 fps on firm blinks the sweep says are caught
  at both) falsifies it the other way and points at the cue-scoring
  window, not the rate.

BRANCH B — NO 60-CAPABLE SETUP. If 13.2's device day finds that no owner
camera negotiates at least 55 fps at or above the 22-pixel ruler — the
ordinary case for a webcam and browser that cap delivery at 30 — then
the 60 fps arm cannot be run at all here. The claim is then UNSCORED and
recorded as a PLATFORM LIMIT, not a refutation: the sweep's mechanism
stands as the deterministic finding it already is, the cued confirmation
waits on hardware that can deliver 60, and this document says so rather
than quietly reporting a 30-only session as if it had tested the rate.
The empty set is a real, publishable outcome, and naming it in advance
is what stops a disappointing device day from being rewritten into a
finding.

HOW THE BRANCH IS CHOSEN. By 13.2's negotiation verdict — a fact about
the hardware, read on the owner's camera day — and by nothing in the
catch rates. That order is the point of committing both branches today:
whether the claim is scored (Branch A) or shelved as a platform limit
(Branch B) is settled by what the cameras can do, before a single cue is
answered, so the data cannot pick the flattering branch after the fact.

The outcome is appended below as a dated section once the sessions run
or the device day closes the door; this prediction is not edited after
either.
