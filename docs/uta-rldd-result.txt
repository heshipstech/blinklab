UTA-RLDD, can blinklab's features classify self-reported drowsiness?
Plan fixed in advance: docs/uta-rldd-plan.md
Full result recorded 3 September 2026. This SUPERSEDES the fold-1 pilot
recorded earlier the same day (a null at DROZY's scale); the pilot's
history is in git and its conclusion is revised here, in writing, exactly
as the plan requires a reversal to be.

HEADLINE
  On the full dataset, blinklab's seven per-second features DETECT
  self-reported drowsiness across strangers, better than chance and past
  the pre-registered label-shuffle control. This REVERSES the prediction.

PREDICTION vs OUTCOME (predict-then-verify)
  The plan committed, before any classifier was fit: "I expect this to
  return a null." That prediction was WRONG. The plan also named the one
  honest route by which it could be wrong: "UTA-RLDD's larger sample
  (roughly 60 subjects against DROZY's 13) is the one way a genuine weak
  signal could surface here that DROZY was too small to see... A
  leave-one-subject-out accuracy well above 1/3 that SURVIVES the shuffle
  would be the surprising, genuine finding, and it would be reported as
  surprising." That is what happened. The fold-1 pilot (12 subjects, about
  DROZY's scale) was a null; the full 54-subject evaluation is a detection.
  A weak effect that needs power to see is the textbook reading, and it is
  reported here as the surprise it is.

THE DATA
  All five folds, measured on the maintainer's machine through the built
  app, classified by analysis/tools/analyse_rldd.py reading NUMBERS ONLY,
  never a frame. Two subjects' drowsy video arrived split across two files
  and were skipped at prep, so 178 of the nominal 180 videos were measured.
  videos measured   178
  excluded          30   (the 25 fps floor, and one short recording)
  analysed          148
  subjects          54   (of 60; six lose all three videos to the fps floor)
  class balance     alert 53, lowvigilant 48, drowsy 47

  EXCLUSION BIAS. The 25 fps floor is aggressive on this phone-recorded
  data: 30 videos and six whole subjects fall below it, the same kind of
  systematic loss DROZY had (16 of 20 there). The surviving classes stay
  close to balanced (53/48/47), and balanced accuracy is used precisely so
  the mild imbalance cannot inflate the score. A result on 54 subjects is
  still far past the pilot's 12 and DROZY's 13.

PRIMARY, three-class leave-one-subject-out
  balanced accuracy   0.498   majority floor 1/3 = 0.333
  imputed cells       22   (in-fold median, for a rare unmeasured feature)
  confusion (rows true, columns predicted):
        alert  lowvi  drows
  alert     40     10      3
  lowvi     24     15      9
  drows     18      9     20
  The signal lives in the extremes: alert recall 40/53 = 0.76 and drowsy
  20/47 = 0.43, with the low-vigilant middle the hardest (15/48 = 0.31),
  exactly the noisiest self-report the plan flagged. Balanced accuracy is
  the mean of the three, 0.498.

NEGATIVE CONTROL, labels shuffled 1000x from the fixed seed, three-class
  observed balanced accuracy   0.498
  null 97.5th percentile       0.407   (mean 0.310)
  permutation p                0.0010
  The shuffled null sits at chance (mean 0.310, near the 1/3 floor), and
  not one of the 1000 shuffles reached the observed 0.498. This is the
  leakage detector doing its job: a null centred at chance means
  leave-one-subject-out left the model NO way to score from subject
  identity, and an observed accuracy far above that null is a real
  feature-label signal, not memorised people.

SECONDARY, alert versus drowsy (the low-vigilant middle dropped)
  balanced accuracy   0.732   majority floor 1/2 = 0.500
  confusion (rows true, columns predicted):
        alert  drows
  alert     46      7
  drows     19     28
  label shuffle: observed 0.732, null 97.5th percentile 0.596 (mean 0.474),
  permutation p 0.0010. The two extremes separate clearly: alert recall
  46/53 = 0.87, drowsy 28/47 = 0.60.

VERDICT, by the rule fixed before the data was seen
    three-class         detecting drowsiness
    alert vs drowsy     detecting drowsiness
  Both clear BOTH pre-registered bars: above the 97.5th percentile of the
  shuffled null (p 0.001) AND above the floor by more than twice the null's
  spread.

VERIFICATION, because a surprising positive is where leakage hides
  The plan warned that "the dishonest route is subject leakage." Four
  independent checks, run before this was recorded:
  1. LEAVE-ONE-SUBJECT-OUT INTEGRITY. Verified on the real 148-video
     matrix: 54 folds, NO subject appears in both the training and the test
     split of any fold, and every video is held out exactly once. A person
     the model scores on was never in its training data.
  2. NULL AT CHANCE. The shuffled-label null is centred on the floor
     (0.310 three-class, 0.474 binary). Had leave-one-subject-out failed to
     block identity leakage, the null would be elevated; it is not.
  3. SEED ROBUSTNESS. The observed accuracy is deterministic -- the model
     has no random seed -- so only the null distribution could move with
     reseeding, and it cannot move far enough: in the recorded 1000-shuffle
     run NOT ONE shuffle reached the observed 0.498 (three-class) or 0.732
     (binary). Two independent reseeds -- 250 shuffles each, seeds 42 and
     2024 -- return the observed unchanged (0.498, 0.732) and a null that
     barely moves: three-class 97.5th percentile 0.397 and 0.393 against
     the recorded run's 0.407, binary 0.575 and 0.609 against 0.596, both
     dimensions clearing the two-bar rule under both seeds. That leaves the
     observed several standard deviations past a null whose 97.5th-percentile
     estimate does not depend on the seed, and neither does the verdict.
  4. WHICH FEATURES CARRY IT. The model leans hardest, in the
     physiologically correct direction, on the drowsiness literature's
     canonical markers: LONG CLOSURES (eyes shut past a blink, the
     microsleep shape) the strongest by far, then PERCLOS (the most
     validated drowsiness proxy there is), then blink duration and rate,
     all pointing "more/longer closure and higher PERCLOS = drowsy", with
     slower lid-closing velocity toward drowsy too. An arbitrary
     recording-condition confound would be unlikely to load specifically
     onto the right features in the right direction; that the weights match
     the physiology is evidence the signal is drowsiness, not framing.

WHAT THIS IS, AND WHAT IT IS NOT
  It IS: on 54 self-recording strangers, seven per-second eyelid features
  classify a coarse self-reported drowsiness state above chance, and the
  binary alert-vs-drowsy contrast does so clearly (0.73 balanced accuracy),
  driven by the physiologically expected features. The accuracy is MODEST,
  not an oracle: the three-class number is 0.50, and the low-vigilant
  middle is barely above chance.
  It is NOT (limits from the plan, unchanged):
  - The label is a coarse, self-reported state; people judge their own
    drowsiness poorly, and that noise caps how high any honest score can go.
  - One video per state per subject means the alert and drowsy videos
    differ in more than drowsiness (time of day, session order, how the
    person sat). The physiologically-sensible feature loadings argue the
    signal is drowsiness rather than such a confound, but this design
    cannot PROVE the separation, and that caveat stands.
  - Self-recorded video in unknown lighting and framing; the frame-rate
    floor is the only such limitation this analysis controls for, and it
    removed six subjects.
  - This is classification across strangers, not causation, not prediction,
    and not driving-relevant drowsiness. Nobody in UTA-RLDD was driving.

RELATION TO THE PRIOR NULLS
  DROZY (13 subjects, correlation with a graded rating) was a null, and the
  fold-1 pilot (12 subjects, this same classifier) was a null. Both are
  consistent with a real but weak effect too small to see at n~12-13. The
  full sample is the first look with the power the plan said was needed, and
  it is where the effect appears. That is the honest shape of a weak signal,
  not a contradiction of the earlier nulls.

WHAT WOULD COUNT AS THIS BEING WRONG
  Stated in advance and still binding. If a later run -- a corrected
  frame-rate floor, a leak found after the fact, or a re-analysis that ties
  the signal to a recording-condition confound rather than drowsiness --
  overturns this, that reversal belongs on this page, in writing, the way
  this result overturned the pilot's. Any positive result a later audit
  traces to subject leakage is retracted here, not quietly dropped.

Temporal Model for Early Drowsiness Detection," CVPR Workshops 2019.
