UTA-RLDD, can blinklab's features classify self-reported drowsiness?
Plan fixed in advance: docs/uta-rldd-plan.md
Result recorded 3 September 2026.

PILOT, read this first
  This is FOLD 1 of UTA-RLDD's five, 12 subjects and 36 videos, run as a
  pilot before committing to the whole dataset. Twelve subjects is about
  DROZY's scale (13), so this is NOT yet the large-sample test the plan was
  powered for: docs/uta-rldd-plan.md argued that UTA-RLDD's ~60 subjects are
  "the one way a genuine weak signal could surface" that DROZY was too small
  to see. A null on fold 1 is a null at DROZY's scale. The full 60-subject
  leave-one-subject-out awaits folds 2 to 5; whether to run them is the
  maintainer's call, recorded alongside this result when made.

PREDICTION, committed before the data (predict-then-verify)
  The plan committed, in writing and before any classifier was fit: "I
  expect this to return a null -- the classifier does not beat chance under
  leave-one-subject-out." On fold 1 that prediction HELD. Both the
  three-class task and the alert-vs-drowsy secondary come back null.

MEASUREMENT
  The 36 videos were flattened and trimmed to their first six minutes by
  analysis/tools/prepare_rldd.py, measured frame-by-frame through the built
  app by tools/measure_corpus.mjs on the maintainer's machine (current app,
  app_commit "dev"), and classified by analysis/tools/analyse_rldd.py. This
  analysis read NUMBERS ONLY: the per-second feature CSVs, never a frame.
  The source and trimmed videos stay on the maintainer's machine and are not
  in this repository; only these aggregate metrics and pseudonymous subject
  ids are recorded, exactly as the permission in DATASETS.md allows.

EXCLUSIONS, a property of the recording fixed before any label
  videos measured   36
  excluded          4
    Fold1part1_06 alert       below 25 fps (median 24.9)
    Fold1part1_06 drowsy      below 25 fps (median 23.9)
    Fold1part2_11 drowsy      under five measured minutes after the settle
    Fold1part2_11 lowvigilant below 25 fps (median 19.7)
  analysed          32
  subjects          12
  class balance     alert 11, lowvigilant 11, drowsy 10

  Three exclusions are the 25 fps floor blinklab needs to measure a blink
  (the same floor that removed 16 of DROZY's 20 sessions); the fourth video
  stopped one second short of the five-minute window and is dropped by both
  reasonable window rules (294 measured in-window seconds, under 300). The
  floor takes drowsy hardest, minus two, so the analysed classes are 11/11/10
  rather than the dataset's balanced 12/12/12. Two subjects (06 and 11) are
  left with a single usable video each. None of these rules were relaxed to
  rescue a borderline clip; bending a threshold after seeing which videos it
  drops is the move the pre-registration exists to forbid.

NEGATIVE CONTROL, labels shuffled 1000 times from the fixed seed, three-class
  observed balanced accuracy   0.248
  null 97.5th percentile       0.491   (mean 0.278)
  permutation p                0.6074
  The observed accuracy sits in the MIDDLE of the shuffled null (60% of
  shuffles beat it). By the plan's control, an accuracy that collapses to its
  shuffled null was never reading drowsiness.

SECONDARY, alert versus drowsy (the low-vigilant middle dropped)
  balanced accuracy   0.614   majority floor 1/2 = 0.500
  confusion (rows true, columns predicted):
        alert  drows
  alert      8      3
  drows      5      5
  label shuffle
    observed balanced accuracy   0.614
    null 97.5th percentile       0.714   (mean 0.452)
    permutation p                0.1718
  This is the instructive one. 0.614 is above the 1/2 floor and, taken
  alone, looks like a weak signal. The shuffle control refuses it: chance
  alone reaches 0.714 at the 97.5th percentile of the null and beats 0.614
  on 17% of shuffles. An apparent margin over the floor that a label shuffle
  erases is not drowsiness; it is the small-sample noise the control exists
  to catch.

VERDICT, by the rule fixed before the data was seen
  a result DETECTS drowsiness only if its accuracy is above the 97.5th
  percentile of the shuffled null AND clears the floor by a margin the
  null's own spread shows is not chance:
    three-class         null: does not beat chance
    alert vs drowsy     null: does not beat chance

  Per the plan, this is the result, published exactly as readily as a
  positive one would have been. On fold 1 of UTA-RLDD, blinklab's seven
  per-second blink and eyelid features do not classify a person's
  self-reported drowsiness state better than chance when no subject appears
  in both the training and the test set.

WHAT THIS CANNOT ANSWER (from the plan, unchanged)
  - The label is a coarse, self-reported three-way state, and people judge
    their own drowsiness poorly; its noise cannot be separated from
    measurement noise.
  - One video per state per subject means the alert and drowsy videos differ
    in more than drowsiness (time of day, session order, how the person
    sat), and the classifier cannot separate drowsiness from those confounds.
  - Self-recorded video in unknown lighting and framing; the frame-rate floor
    is the only limitation this analysis controls for.
  - This is fold 1 only. A weak signal that DROZY (13 subjects) and this
    pilot (12) are both too small to see remains possible in the full
    60-subject dataset; that is the one honest route to a positive, and it
    needs folds 2 to 5. It is NOT a route this pilot has closed.

WHAT WOULD COUNT AS THIS BEING WRONG (from the plan, unchanged)
  If a later run -- the remaining folds, a corrected frame-rate floor, or a
  leak found after the fact -- reverses this conclusion, that reversal
  belongs on this page, in the same way the three Eyeblink8 numbers do. Any
  positive result a later audit traces to subject leakage is retracted here,
  in writing, not quietly dropped.
