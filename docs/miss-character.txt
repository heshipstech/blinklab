THE MISS CHARACTER QUESTION: ARE THEY THE SAME BLINKS?
======================================================

Written 27 August 2026, before any overlap is computed. The README's
miss paragraph ends on an open question: 47 of the 67 missed blinks
contain a frame the human marked fully closed, "that is a real
weakness and nobody has explained it yet". Explaining WHY needs a
frame-level aperture trace around each miss, which the exports do
not carry and a future instrument increment will have to add. What
CAN be answered today, from tables already committed, is a prior
question that shapes that whole investigation: is the instrument
missing THE SAME blinks run after run, or a shifting population of
the same size?

THE TABLES, all committed under docs/evidence/, one row per missed
blink, joined on (clip, blink_id) — the corpus annotation's own
identity, stable across runs by construction:

    2026-08-09/tables-current-run   50 misses   capfix era, ratchet ruler
    2026-08-20-frozen               67 misses   frozen ruler
    2026-08-21-rearm                67 misses   frozen ruler, the
                                                published run

WHY IDENTITY IS A STRONGER CLAIM THAN COUNT. Every reproduction this
project has published — including the 26 August digit-for-digit
confirmation — compares COUNTS: found, missed, false, per clip. Two
runs can match every count while missing different blinks, and no
published number would notice. The committed miss tables make the
identity question decidable for the misses, and it has never been
asked.

THE PREDICTIONS, committed before anything is joined.

  1. FROZEN vs REARM: the two 67-row tables are IDENTICAL sets, all
     67 shared. The rearm change (a deterministic clock, #189) was
     proven measurement-neutral at count level; if it moved even one
     miss identity while keeping every count, that is a finding the
     count-level A/B could not see, and it is published as one.

  2. CAPFIX vs REARM: at least 40 of the capfix era's 50 misses (80
     percent) persist in the rearm 67. The freeze was predicted and
     measured to LOWER recall — a frozen line late in a clip demands
     deeper dips — so the rearm set should be mostly the capfix set
     plus blinks the freeze newly lost, not a reshuffle.

  3. THE PERSISTENT CORE CARRIES THE CLOSED-FRAME CHARACTER: among
     the misses present in BOTH the capfix and rearm tables, the
     share containing a fully-closed frame is within a few points of
     the overall 70.1 percent — the weakness the README names is a
     stable property of particular blinks, not an artefact of run
     churn.

WHAT EACH OUTCOME CHANGES.

  - Prediction 1 wrong: the determinism claim gets a dated caveat
    everywhere it is published (counts reproduce, identities do
    not), and the miss investigation must treat every run's miss
    list as a sample rather than a census.
  - Prediction 2 wrong in the LOW direction (large reshuffle): the
    README's sentence that freezing "changed how many blinks are
    missed and not the character of what is missed" is corrected in
    the open — aggregate shares hid an identity-level reshaping.
  - Prediction 3 wrong: the closed-frame weakness concentrates in
    the churn, which would point the frame-level investigation at
    the ruler's late-clip behaviour rather than at the aperture
    signal itself.

HOW IT IS SCORED. A committed tool, analysis/tools/miss_overlap.py,
joins two miss tables on (clip, blink_id) and prints the set sizes,
the intersection, each difference, and the fully-closed-frame share
of each group. Its tests are watched failing first, on synthetic
tables. The outcomes are appended below as a dated section, and
these predictions are not edited.

THE OUTCOMES, 27 August 2026, all three predictions confirmed
--------------------------------------------------------------

FROZEN vs REARM: identical sets, all 67 shared, zero in either
difference. Prediction 1 holds, and the determinism claim is now an
identity-level fact for the misses: the rearm run did not just
reproduce the counts, it missed exactly the same blinks.

CAPFIX vs REARM: 46 of the capfix era's 50 misses persist — 92
percent, above prediction 2's 80 — with 21 misses added by the
freeze and 4 recovered. The rearm miss list is the capfix list plus
what the frozen line newly lost, not a reshuffle.

THE CHARACTER RIDES THE CORE, AND THE CHURN TOO. The persistent 46
carry a closed-frame share of 69.6 percent against the overall 70.1
— prediction 3 holds. The 21 freeze-added misses carry 71.4 percent,
the same character again, and the 4 recovered were all closed-frame
(four rows, so a note rather than a number). The README's sentence
that freezing changed "how many blinks are missed and not the
character of what is missed" now has identity-level support, not
only aggregate shares.

WHAT THIS SETS UP. The frame-level "why" investigation has a fixed
target now: a persistent core of 46 blinks, missed under two
different ruler regimes and two different runs, seventy percent of
them containing a frame a human marked fully closed. Whatever
explanation is found must explain THOSE blinks, and an instrument
increment that adds a per-frame aperture trace around a miss is the
tool that investigation needs first.

THE AUTOPSY, PREDICTED, 2 September 2026
---------------------------------------

Written before the classifier exists and before any trace is read,
in the predict-then-verify discipline. docs/miss-trace.txt named the
three stories a per-frame trace can separate; this increment builds
the tool that assigns each miss to one, and commits here what the
tool is expected to find when the awake run's traces finally feed
it. The classifier refuses to name a mechanism the trace cannot
show: with no trace it reports only what the committed miss table
already knows (closed-frame or shallow); with a trace it decides.

THE FOUR VERDICTS the tool assigns to a miss, from the trace rows
whose frameIndex falls inside the annotation's own [startFrame,
endFrame] closed span (no chosen margin — the annotation's own
boundary):

  not_measured   no trace row covers the closed span at all: the
                 closure fell where the instrument produced no frame
                 (the sampling story). A stepped run measures every
                 frame, so this should be near-empty, and the trace
                 proves it.
  no_trusted_face  the span has frames but the model returned no
                 trusted face on any of them (aperture null
                 throughout): a trust failure, a kind of landmark
                 story.
  crossed_line   some measured frame in the span read below the
                 effective blink line, yet no blink was logged: the
                 aperture signal was there and the detector's own
                 state machine (re-arm, refractory) swallowed it.
  above_line     every measured frame stayed at or above the blink
                 line: the signal never crossed. The unexplained
                 core. For these the tool reports min_ratio, the
                 smallest apertureMm / blinkLineMm over the span:
                 near 1.0 means the aperture came close (a
                 threshold-margin story); well above 1.0 means it
                 barely moved (a landmark story, the model's lids
                 not following the real ones). The tool draws no
                 tuned line between those two — it reports the
                 measured margin and lets the distribution speak.

THE PREDICTION, over the published 67 misses (and the persistent 46
within them), scored against the awake run's trace:

  1. above_line DOMINATES: at least 40 of the 67, and at least 30 of
     the 47 closed-frame misses, never crossed the blink line.
  2. Among those above_line misses the min_ratio clusters WELL above
     1.0 — median at least 1.5 — i.e. the aperture barely moved on
     frames a human marked fully closed. The missed blinks are a
     LANDMARK failure: the model's aperture does not follow the real
     lids down.
  3. not_measured is near zero (at most 2): a stepped run leaves
     almost no coverage gap, so sampling is not the story.
  4. crossed_line is a minority (at most 10): the state machine
     accounts for a few, not the core.

WHAT EACH OUTCOME REDIRECTS. If instead min_ratio hugs 1.0, the line
is merely too strict (a threshold-margin fix, and cheap). If
crossed_line dominates, the signal is fine and the re-arm/refractory
logic is the bug. If not_measured is large, stepped coverage is not
what the runner claims. Only the predicted landmark outcome points
at the hard problem — the aperture signal itself on a real closure —
and would gate the deferred recall change on a second, independent
closure measure (the iris-occlusion signal) before any line moves.
This prediction is not edited after the trace is read; the outcome
becomes a new dated section below it.


THE AUTOPSY, VERIFIED, 2 September 2026
---------------------------------------

The awake corpus trace run happened, on the project's new machine,
and the classifier ran over its per-frame traces. First, the control
the prediction did not ask for but the run supplies for free: the run
reproduced the SAME 67 misses, blink-for-blink, as the committed
2026-08-21-rearm table, on a different CPU, OS and browser build. The
miss set is hardware-independent; the single-machine results it rests
on were not an artefact of that machine. The verdict table is
docs/evidence/2026-09-02-awake-autopsy/eyeblink8_miss_autopsy.csv.

The mechanism split, over the 67:

  not_measured       0    closed-frame share -
  no_trusted_face    4    closed-frame share 75.0%
  crossed_line      29    closed-frame share 96.6%
  above_line        34    closed-frame share 47.1%
  above_line min_ratio: closest 1.02, median 1.24, farthest 1.66

The prediction, scored:

  1. above_line DOMINATES (>= 40 of 67, >= 30 of the 47 closed-frame
     misses). REFUTED. above_line is 34, a plurality not a
     dominance, and only 16 of the 47 closed-frame misses are
     above_line.
  2. above_line min_ratio median >= 1.5, a landmark failure.
     REFUTED. The median is 1.24 and the closest is 1.02: where the
     aperture stayed above the line it stayed close to it, not well
     clear of it. The lids were not barely moving.
  3. not_measured <= 2. CONFIRMED, at 0. A stepped run leaves no
     coverage gap; sampling is not the story, exactly as claimed.
  4. crossed_line <= 10, a minority. REFUTED, badly, at 29 — the
     largest single mechanism, and 28 of its 29 contain a frame a
     human marked fully closed.

Three of four wrong, and the central one — landmark failure — wrong
in the direction its own redirect map named. Of the 47 misses where a
human saw a fully closed frame, 28 (60 percent) are crossed_line: the
instrument's aperture DID dip below the effective blink line and no
blink was logged. The signal was present and correct; the detector's
own re-arm/refractory state machine swallowed it. That is the
prediction file's "if crossed_line dominates, the signal is fine and
the re-arm/refractory logic is the bug" branch, and it is where the
largest share of the recall deficit now points.

The above_line group (34, but only 47 percent closed-frame, median
min_ratio 1.24) is the prediction file's OTHER cheap branch: the line
is somewhat too strict, and about half of these are shallow misses
with no fully-closed frame at all — genuinely partial closures rather
than a perception failure. no_trusted_face (4, three of them
closed-frame) is the only landmark-shaped bucket, and it is tiny: the
model lost the face on a handful of real closures.

WHAT THIS REDIRECTS. The deferred recall change was gated, IF the
landmark outcome held, on a second independent closure measure (the
iris-occlusion signal) before any line moved. The landmark outcome did
not hold, so that gate is off the critical path. The recall work now
has two detector-logic targets, in order of size: first the
re-arm/refractory state machine, which drops a signal that is already
below the line (crossed_line, 29, and 28 of the 47 real closures);
second the blink-line threshold, which is marginally too strict for
part of the above_line group. Both are code, not model — cheaper and
more tractable than the landmark failure this section predicted and
did not find. The next increment is the state-machine one: reproduce a
crossed_line miss against the real detector in the trace harness, and
show why a frame below the line logs no blink.


THE STATE-MACHINE REPRODUCTION, 2 September 2026
------------------------------------------------

The VERIFIED section named crossed_line the largest mechanism and the
re-arm/refractory state machine the bug. Reproducing it against the
real detector refines that, and partly walks it back. The 20 short
crossed_line misses (frameLength <= 15, so under MAX_BLINK_DURATION;
the other 9 are the long-closure story) had their raw per-frame
aperture-and-line slices replayed through src/core/blink.ts's
blinkStep. Attribution in
docs/evidence/2026-09-02-awake-autopsy/crossed_line_gate_attribution.csv.

The detector does not arm a blink candidate when the aperture merely
crosses the line. Since fix #114 it arms only when the aperture
reaches the ARM line, a hysteresis gap below the threshold:
apertureMm <= line * (1 - APERTURE_HYSTERESIS_FRACTION), i.e.
<= 0.9 * line. The autopsy's crossed_line verdict fires at the raw
line (1.0 * line). Between them sits a band, [0.9, 1.0) * line, that
is "below the line" to the autopsy but "too shallow to be a blink" to
the detector. That band is where the misses live:

  never_armed (min_ratio 0.91 to 0.996):   15 of 20
  armed_then_dropped (min_ratio 0.26 to 0.45):  5 of 20

The never_armed 15 are init-independent and robust: whether the
aperture reaches 0.9 * line is a property of the aperture alone, not
of any state a 2-second slice cannot see. They are the fix #114 depth
gate doing exactly what test/core/blink.test.ts already pins ("refuses
a real but too-shallow closure, the documented cost"). The 5
armed_then_dropped are the genuine state-machine drops — deep
closures that armed and were still not counted — and are the
re-arm/refractory bug; a 2-second slice cannot always say which of the
two, and for one (b30) the sim even miscounts because the causing
blink predates the window, so they are recorded as a class, not
individually adjudicated.

WHAT THIS CORRECTS. The state machine is not the largest lever; it is
about 5 misses. Fold the 15 never_armed back in with the 34 above_line
(which never went below the line at all) and about 49 of the 67 misses
share one behaviour: on a frame a human marked fully closed, the
measured aperture never dropped decisively — it hovered within roughly
a tenth to a quarter of the line, where a truly shut eye should read
an aperture near zero. The VERIFIED section read the raw-line
crossed_line count as a state-machine signal; against the detector's
own arm line, most of it is the same shallow-aperture population as
above_line, split by an accident of where the autopsy drew its line.

WHAT THIS REDIRECTS. The shallow-aperture population is either the
personal line placed too high or the aperture landmark under-reading a
real closure, and those two are separable only by a second,
independent closure measure. The VERIFIED section put the
iris-occlusion signal off the critical path because it read the result
as state-machine; the reproduction puts it back ON, as the instrument
that adjudicates the 49. The next build is that signal, ahead of its
data, with its own committed prediction: does an independent closure
measure register a closure on the frames where the aperture did not?
The 5-miss state-machine drop stays a real but minor side-fix, not the
lead.
