THE VELOCITY SAMPLING BIAS, AND A PREDICTION
============================================

Written 7 September 2026 (roadmap 10.10c4b, ladder B12, audit F-033),
before any number below is computed, in the predict-then-verify
discipline this repository requires of anything it measures.

WHY IT EXISTS
-------------
The page publishes a peak closing velocity in millimetres per second
and an amplitude-over-velocity time constant derived from it, and the
plan for both says the ratio "grows with drowsiness: tired lids lose
speed before they lose travel". Neither figure carries a bound on how
much the frame rate alone moves it.

It moves it, and the reason is in the arithmetic rather than in the
eye. src/core/blinkShape.ts computes the peak as the largest drop
between two ADJACENT samples divided by their interval: one forward
finite difference. A finite difference does not measure the fastest
instant of a descent, it measures the average speed over one sampling
interval that happens to contain it. The faster the lid and the longer
the interval, the more of the peak is averaged away.

A human blink's closing phase is roughly 50 to 120 milliseconds. At 25
frames per second the interval is 40 ms and at 30 it is 33 ms, so a
single interval covers a third to most of the whole descent. That is
not a correction term, it is the same order as the thing being
measured, and every velocity this project has published was measured
that way.

THE METHOD, FIXED NOW
---------------------
A pure simulation in src/core, in the shape samplingBounds.ts already
uses for the PERCLOS bound: a synthetic descent with a known true peak
velocity, sampled at a stated rate, run through the SAME arithmetic
blinkShape.ts uses, and the underestimate reported as a fraction of
the truth.

The descent is a raised-cosine fall from open to shut over a stated
closing duration: aperture(t) = open - amplitude * (1 - cos(pi t / T))
/ 2 for t in [0, T]. It is chosen because it is smooth, has a single
interior velocity peak, and has an analytic peak of pi * amplitude /
(2T), so the truth needs no second simulation to establish. A real lid
is not a raised cosine; what carries over is the shape of the bias,
not a per-person correction, and this document may not be read as one.

Sampling phase matters and is not averaged away by luck: a sampler
that happens to straddle the peak reads higher than one that does not.
So each rate is evaluated at many evenly spaced starting offsets
across one interval, and the WORST, BEST and MEDIAN underestimate are
all reported. A single phase would understate the spread a real
recording sees.

Rates: 25, 30, 60 and 240 frames per second. Closing durations: 50,
80 and 120 ms. The last rate is far above anything this project runs
and is there as the control: if the bias does not go to nearly zero
at 240, the simulation is wrong rather than the instrument.

THE PREDICTION
--------------
Written before running it.

1. The bias is a systematic UNDERESTIMATE of peak velocity at every
   rate and every phase. There is no sampling of a smooth descent that
   reads faster than its true peak, so a positive bias anywhere would
   mean the simulation or the arithmetic is wrong.

2. At 25 to 30 frames per second, over the 50 to 120 ms descents, the
   median underestimate lands between 10 and 40 percent, and the
   audit's reproduced figure of 12 to 28 percent sits inside that
   band. If the measured band excludes 12 to 28 percent, the audit and
   this simulation disagree and the row stops to find out why rather
   than publishing either.

3. The bias grows as the descent shortens and as the interval
   lengthens, monotonically in both. A 50 ms descent at 25 fps is the
   worst cell in the table; a 120 ms descent at 240 fps is the best.

4. At 240 frames per second the median underestimate is below 2
   percent for every duration.

5. Amplitude-over-velocity is biased the opposite way and by
   essentially the same factor, because amplitude is barely touched by
   sampling while velocity is divided into it. A ratio published as a
   drowsiness time constant therefore reads LONGER than the truth on a
   slow machine, which is the same direction drowsiness itself moves
   it. That is the finding this row exists for, and it is stated as a
   prediction here so it cannot be discovered after the fact.

WHAT THIS ROW DOES NOT DO
-------------------------
It does not correct the published numbers. A correction needs a
descent model fitted to real lids, and this simulation is a raised
cosine chosen for its analytic peak. The correction is roadmap C6.
This row measures the bound and states it where the numbers are
defined and published.

PREDICTION vs OUTCOME
---------------------
1. CORRECT. No cell at any phase read faster than the truth.

2. HALF WRONG, and the wrong half is mine. The band predicted was 10
   to 40 percent at 25 and 30 frames per second over 50 to 120 ms
   descents. The measured band is 5.4 to 36.8. The ceiling held and
   the floor did not.

   The reason: I estimated the loss as if one sampling interval fell
   wherever it fell. analyzeClosing takes the LARGEST drop among all
   adjacent pairs on the descent, so at any phase it already picks the
   best-placed interval available, and the loss is smaller than a
   fixed-interval estimate suggests. The effect is largest where there
   are most intervals to choose between, which is why the two 120 ms
   cells are the ones that fell out of the predicted band.

   The audit's reproduced 12 to 28 percent does sit inside the
   measured spread, so the second half of prediction 2 held and the
   two measurements do not disagree.

3. CORRECT. The bias falls monotonically as the descent lengthens and
   as the interval shortens, in every row and every column.

4. CORRECT. At 240 frames per second every median is at or below 0.5
   percent, well inside the 2 percent predicted.

5. CORRECT, and exactly. At 25 frames per second over an 80 ms
   descent the ratio is multiplied by 1.202 while the velocity loses
   16.8 percent, and 1 / (1 - 0.168) = 1.202 to three figures.
   Amplitude's own loss to sampling is second order, as predicted.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR A PUBLISHED NUMBER
--------------------------------------
A blink's closing phase near the short end of the human range, on a
machine running at 25 frames per second, can have its peak velocity
published at under half its true value: 60.2 percent low at the worst
sampling phase, 36.8 percent at the median. The same session's
amplitude-over-velocity reads up to 1.58 times longer than the truth.

That last direction is the one to watch. Drowsiness lengthens the
ratio, and so does a slow computer, by an amount of the same order.
Two people on different machines are not comparable on this figure,
and one person compared against themselves is only comparable while
their frame rate holds.

None of this is corrected here. A correction needs a descent model
fitted to real lids rather than a raised cosine chosen for its
analytic peak, and that is roadmap C6. What this row does is measure
the bound and state it where the numbers are defined and published.
