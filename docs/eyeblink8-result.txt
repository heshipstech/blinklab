HOW TO REPRODUCE THIS EXACTLY

    cd analysis
    PYTHONPATH="$PWD" .venv/bin/python tools/evaluate_eyeblink8.py \
        "$DATASETS/eyeblink8/eyeblink8" \
        "$DATASETS/eyeblink8-measured-refractory"

THIS RUN REPEATS. Measuring one clip three times produces identical
files, byte for byte. No earlier figure on this page did, and that is
the most important property of this run. Before pull request #189 the
app handed the face model a wall clock reading, and the model uses the
gap between readings when it follows a face from frame to frame, so how
busy the computer was leaked into the measurement.

THREE PUBLISHED NUMBERS, ALL KEPT

  69.6% recall, F1 77.1   the export deleted its own rows (#172)
  82.8% recall, F1 84.6   export fixed, clock still wobbled (#173)
  87.7% recall, F1 85.4   clock fixed (#189), double counting cut (#190)

Precision fell from 86.4% to 83.3% between the second and the third and
that is not a regression. The deterministic clock made the detector more
sensitive: 20 more real blinks found, and far more of the same blink
reported twice. The refractory period of #190 then removed 39 of those
false alarms at ZERO cost to recall.

THE REMAINING 72 FALSE ALARMS

  45 of 72 sit on a real blink under the strict rule, no widening.
  64 of 72 under the four frame tolerance rule.
  61 of 72 are three frames long or shorter.

They are the same phenomenon as the ones removed: fragments of real
blinks, sitting further than 150 ms from the blink they belong to.
Raising the refractory period to 300 ms would catch most of them. It
stays at 150, which is below the ~200 ms a person can produce
deliberately. A constant chosen to improve a score on a benchmark
already read is fitting, not measuring.
