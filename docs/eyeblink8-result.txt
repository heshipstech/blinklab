THIS NUMBER IS LOWER THAN THE ONE IT REPLACES, ON PURPOSE

The previous run scored 87.7% recall, and part of that score was
earned by a ruler that moved. The baseline the blink line is half of
was allowed to rise during a session, and measured on that run it had
risen on EVERY one of the eight clips, by 2.3 to 37.6 percent, so the
line grew more permissive as each clip went on. The six-person
validation round then failed its pre-registered baseline criterion on
exactly that behaviour: a volunteer's ruler rose 34.6 percent during
the scripted measurement. On 20 August 2026 the ruler was frozen at
its thirty-second calibration (docs/baseline-freeze.txt, predictions
committed before this run), and this is the same detector measured
with the ruler held still. The recall predicted to land between 82
and 86 landed at 83.6. Verified from this run's own records: the
baseline's drift is 0.0 percent on all eight clips, and every birth
value is identical to the previous run's, so the delta is the freeze
and nothing else.

One prediction was WRONG and stays in the record: precision was
predicted to hold or rise, and it fell, 83.3 to 81.4, because
invented detections ROSE from 72 to 78. The mechanism, found by
diffing the two runs: at the frozen, lower line, near-line flutter
fragments into multiple crossings 200 to 400 ms apart, the same
re-crossing signature the validation round's P1 produced live. Five
of the six new false alarms sit in one clip's flutter episode. The
re-arm investigation (docs/blink-rearm.txt) targets exactly that
signature.

THIS RUN REPEATS. Measuring one clip three times produces identical
files, byte for byte, a property held since pull request #189.

Precision fell from 86.4% to 83.3% between the second and the third,
and that was not a regression: the deterministic clock made the
detector more sensitive and the refractory period then removed 39
false alarms at zero cost to recall. The fall from 87.7% to 83.6%
between the third and the fourth is not a regression either: it is
the same detector scored by a ruler that no longer moves mid-clip,
which is the only honest way to score it after what the validation
round proved about moving rulers.

THE 78 FALSE ALARMS OF THIS RUN

  49 of 78 sit on a real blink under the strict rule, no widening.
  66 of 78 under the four frame tolerance rule.
  64 of 78 are three frames long or shorter.

The same phenomenon as before the freeze, and slightly more of it:
fragments of real blinks and near-line flutter, sitting further than
150 ms from the event they belong to. Raising the refractory period
to 300 ms would catch most of them and stays off the table: a
constant chosen to improve a score on a benchmark already read is
fitting, not measuring. The re-arm gate under investigation attacks
the mechanism instead, and its predictions were committed before its
experiment ran.
