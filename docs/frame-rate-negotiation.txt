FRAME-RATE NEGOTIATION: THE ADOPTION CONDITION, WRITTEN FIRST
=============================================================

Written 10 September 2026, before any owned device is read under
the new request, in the predict-then-verify discipline. Roadmap row
13.2, brief C1 — the era's other real lever, and the one this
project has never pulled: in every session in the record, every
camera declared 30.0 frames per second, because the page has never
ASKED for more (src/io/camera.ts requests a resolution and no frame
rate at all, a fact docs/blink-sample-rate.txt has stated since
August).

WHY 60 IS WORTH ASKING FOR
--------------------------
The committed sampling model (docs/blink-sample-rate.txt) puts the
catch probability for a blink at the 3.40 mm minimum at 0.48 when
the camera delivers 30 frames per second and 0.96 when it delivers
60. The dry run's field data agrees from its own direction: the two
~30 fps sessions missed 3 and 1 of ten deliberate blinks, the 55
and 126.7 processing sessions caught 10 of 10 — with the 27 August
correction standing, that the rate that decides is the rate of
DISTINCT frames read. Doubling delivery is the one change the model
says roughly doubles the odds of catching a minimal blink.

WHAT THE CODE WILL DO — A MEASUREMENT, NOT AN ASSUMPTION
--------------------------------------------------------
After the camera is granted and before measurement begins:

1. Read the live track's getCapabilities(): the DECLARED maximum
   frame rate (and width/height bounds), recorded even when the ask
   fails, because "the camera says it can do 60 and did not" and
   "the camera never claimed 60" are different facts.
2. Record the track's settings BEFORE the ask: the negotiated rate
   and resolution the default request produced.
3. applyConstraints({ frameRate: { ideal: 60 } }) — ideal, never
   exact, so a camera that cannot comply returns what it can and
   nothing breaks.
4. Record settings AFTER: the negotiated rate and resolution.

The request also gains facingMode: { ideal: "user" }, per the row:
a phone offered no preference has been free to hand over either
camera, and the instrument means to watch the person holding it.

The whole step is written down in the export header per session —
declared maximum, rate before, rate after, resolution before and
after — and A RESOLUTION CHANGE IS SURFACED, never silent: the
model's iris ruler runs on source pixels, so a camera that trades
1920x1080@30 down to 1280x720@60 has moved the instrument's
precision, and the record must say so wherever the rate gain is
claimed.

THE ADOPTION CONDITION, COMMITTED BEFORE ANY DEVICE IS READ
-----------------------------------------------------------
The ask ships unconditionally as a measurement. What the owner's
device day decides is what may be CLAIMED from it, and the
condition is fixed now:

- A setup counts as 60-CAPABLE only if the ask negotiates a
  measured rate of at least 55 frames per second (the dry run's own
  10-of-10 threshold, not a round number) AND the negotiated
  resolution keeps the iris ruler at or above the smallest iris the
  record has published a sound session at — 22 pixels, the MacBook
  Air's — at the owner's ordinary sitting distance. Rate bought by
  resolution the ruler cannot afford is not a capability, it is a
  trade, and it is recorded as one.
- If NO owned camera meets that condition, the honest conclusion is
  the one the row names: live accuracy on owned hardware is capped
  at 30 frames per second, the capability ladder (13.6a) says so
  per setup, and the 60 fps branch of the sampling model stays a
  model. That outcome is as publishable as the other.
- The per-device numbers land in a dated section below this one:
  declared maximum, negotiated rate, any resolution change, per
  owned device. Until that section exists, no sentence anywhere in
  this repository claims a delivered rate above 30.

THE PREDICTIONS
---------------
P1. At least one owned camera's getCapabilities() declares a
    maximum of 60 or more (modern webcams commonly do), so the
    interesting question will be what applyConstraints DOES, not
    what the driver claims.
P2. The Sony rig through the Cam Link (the pcsony setup) negotiates
    60 at 1080p: capture cards commonly expose 1080p60, and that
    rig already processed at 126.7.
P3. The built-in FaceTime camera holds 30 whatever is asked, or
    yields 60 only below 1080p — either way it fails the adoption
    condition at 1080p.
P4. On every device, the stepped corpus is untouched by
    construction: a clip has no track to negotiate with, and the
    change lives entirely on the camera path.

THE FALSIFIERS, NAMED BEFORE THE NUMBERS
----------------------------------------
If a camera negotiates 60 while getCapabilities() declared 30, the
capabilities read is not trustworthy on that platform and the
record says which. If the ask CHANGES the resolution silently —
settings after differing from before with no surfaced record — the
builder test has failed at its one job and the code does not ship.
If asking for 60 breaks the stream outright on any device (black
frames, zero delivery), the ask is demoted from unconditional to
opt-in and this section records why.

Whichever way the device day lands, its outcome becomes a dated
section below, and row 13.2 ticks on that section — not on this
build.
