THE FALSE-ALARM CHARACTER QUESTION: WHAT DID THE DETECTOR SEE?
=============================================================

Written 13 September 2026, before the anchor run's false-alarm table
is read. The miss autopsy (docs/miss-character.txt) asked, of the 67
blinks a human saw and the instrument did not, what the aperture was
doing on those frames. This is its mirror: the instrument logged 65
blinks the human annotated NOTHING at, and the same mechanism
treatment is owed to them. A recall deficit and a precision deficit
are separate faults with separate fixes, and neither is understood by
its count alone.

WHY THE MECHANISM QUESTION MATTERS MORE FOR A FALSE ALARM THAN THE
COUNT DOES. The tempting reading of 65 false alarms is that the
detector is trigger-happy and the blink line sits too low, and the
fix is to raise it. Since fix #114 that reading is almost certainly
wrong, and it costs recall to act on. A blink is only LOGGED when the
aperture plunges past the ARM line — thresholdMm * (1 -
APERTURE_HYSTERESIS_FRACTION), i.e. 0.9 of the personal line
(src/core/blink.ts, src/core/constants.ts: APERTURE_HYSTERESIS_FRACTION
= 0.1) — and then reopens within MAX_BLINK_DURATION_MS (500 ms), no
sooner than BLINK_REFRACTORY_MS (150 ms) after the last count, and
only after the eye cleared the RE-ARM line at 1.1 of the threshold.
Noise riding a tenth of a millimetre below the line no longer mints a
blink; fix #114 was measured to kill exactly that. So a false alarm in
this era is not line-riding chatter. It is a GENUINE, DEEP, BRIEF
aperture plunge on a frame the ground truth marked empty. The
question the count cannot answer is what made the aperture plunge
there, and the honest possibilities point at different fixes that do
not all cost recall.

THE FACTS EACH FALSE ALARM CARRIES, from which its mechanism is read
(the columns the table already joins, from the detection, the
annotation, and the run's per-second seconds.csv):

  framesToNearestAnnotatedBlink  frames from this detection to the
                 closest annotated blink, 0 when they overlap. A small
                 value means the false alarm hugs a real blink.
  overlapsNF     whether any frame of the detection's span fell on a
                 frame the annotation marks non-frontal ("N"). A turned
                 head foreshortens the eye and can read shut.
  beforeBaselineReady  whether the detection fired before the second
                 at which the personal baseline first replaced the
                 fixed 4 mm fallback line. An un-calibrated line is a
                 different instrument.
  faceDetectedAtThatSecond, apertureMmAtThatSecond,
  baselineMmAtThatSecond  the per-second state at the detection's
                 start second: whether a trusted face was present, and
                 how deep the aperture sat relative to the line.

THE FOUR VERDICTS the tool assigns to each false alarm, PRIORITY-
ORDERED so a false alarm that could be told more than one story is
named by the first that fits (the same if/elif discipline miss_autopsy
uses). Order is a claim about which story dominates the others when
both are present, not a ranking of severity.

  near_annotation   framesToNearestAnnotatedBlink <= the refractory
                 window in frames (150 ms at 30 fps rounds to 5). The
                 detector fired one blink's width from a real one:
                 either a genuine blink split into two counts, or a
                 reopening wobble the matcher would not pair. This is a
                 MATCHING/state-machine story, not a bad detection of
                 nothing — the aperture that fired here belongs to a
                 real blink next door.
  non_frontal    overlapsNF is set (and it is not near_annotation). The
                 aperture crossed while the head was turned, where a
                 foreshortened eye reads shallower than it is: a POSE
                 story, and the lever is a pose gate, not the line.
  before_baseline  the detection fired before baselineReady (and is
                 neither of the above). The line it crossed was the
                 fixed fallback, not the person's own: a PREPARATION
                 story. This is the DROPPABLE sub-question (below).
  deep_unannotated  none of the above. A deep, brief aperture plunge on
                 a trusted, frontal, calibrated frame the annotation
                 left empty. The residue: either a real closure the
                 ground truth did not record, or a real artefact that
                 is not pose and not preparation. For this group the
                 tool reports the aperture depth it can see —
                 apertureMmAtThatSecond / baselineMmAtThatSecond, the
                 per-second ratio, labelled as per-second and not the
                 frame minimum, because the committed table carries the
                 second and not yet the frame. A ratio near or below
                 the arm line's 0.9 is a genuinely deep closure the
                 annotator missed; well above it is a per-second
                 average hiding a briefer frame-level dip, which only
                 the trace can resolve.

PREPARATION-SENSITIVITY, THE DROPPABLE SUB-QUESTION. before_baseline
asks whether the detector mints phantom blinks in the window before
the personal line is set, on the 4 mm fallback. It is named droppable
on purpose: if a run's seconds tables do not carry a clean
baselineReady second for every clip, or if the count is negligible,
this verdict collapses into deep_unannotated and the rest of the split
stands unharmed. The other three verdicts do not depend on it. A row
that cannot answer its hardest sub-question should say which one it
dropped and keep the answers it can defend, rather than fail whole.

THE PREDICTIONS, over the anchor run's 65 false alarms, committed
before that table is read:

  1. deep_unannotated is the PLURALITY, at least 30 of the 65. Since
     fix #114 the detector only fires on deep crossings, so most false
     alarms are real deep dips the ground truth did not mark. If this
     holds, the precision deficit is as much an annotation-
     completeness question as a detector fault, and desensitising the
     line to erase these would cost recall for no measured gain.
  2. non_frontal is a real minority but not the largest single
     mechanism: between 5 and 20 of the 65. Eyeblink8 carries labelled
     non-frontal frames, and a turned head is a known way to read a
     shallow eye as shut, but it is not expected to account for the
     bulk.
  3. near_annotation is at most 12. The re-arm gate (20 August 2026,
     docs/blink-rearm.txt) and the 150 ms refractory already suppress
     the reopen-wobble double count, so few false alarms should sit one
     blink's width from a real one. If near_annotation is instead
     large, the re-arm/refractory logic still splits blinks and the
     lever is that state machine, not the line — the same conclusion
     the miss autopsy reached from the other side.
  4. before_baseline is small, at most 8, and is reported with the
     droppable caveat above. Most clips settle the personal baseline
     inside the first tens of seconds and false alarms distribute
     across the whole clip, so the preparation window should hold few.
     If it is instead large, the fallback line is mis-set for these
     subjects and gating detection on baselineReady is a cheap,
     recall-neutral fix — which would promote the droppable sub-
     question to the lead.

WHAT EACH OUTCOME REDIRECTS.

  - Prediction 1 wrong in the LOW direction (deep_unannotated is a
    minority): the false alarms are mostly pose, preparation, or
    matching artefacts, each with its own recall-neutral fix, and the
    precision work becomes a sum of small targeted gates rather than
    one question about the line or the ground truth.
  - Prediction 2 wrong HIGH (non_frontal dominates): a pose gate that
    refuses an aperture reading on a non-frontal frame is the single
    largest precision lever, and it costs no recall on frontal blinks.
  - Prediction 3 wrong HIGH (near_annotation dominates): the detector
    is still splitting real blinks despite the re-arm gate; the fix is
    in the state machine and would also change the blink COUNT, so it
    is measured against recall before it lands.
  - Prediction 4 wrong HIGH (before_baseline dominates): the
    preparation window is where the phantoms live, and the droppable
    sub-question becomes the headline — gate detection until
    baselineReady.

HOW IT IS SCORED. A committed tool, built as the sibling of
analysis/tools/write_miss_table.py (the table writer) and
analysis/tools/miss_autopsy.py (the classifier), regenerates the
anchor's 65-row false-alarm table from the run's annotations and blink
logs, then assigns each row one of the four verdicts above and prints
the split and, for deep_unannotated, the per-second depth ratios. The
tool's tests are watched failing first, on SYNTHETIC .tag / .blinks.csv
fixtures, because the anchor corpus and its measured folder are the
owner's; the writer REFUSES a clip whose blink log is missing or was
measured in any mode but stepped, rather than writing a shorter table,
the same refusal write_miss_table.py already makes. The verdict CSV is
committed under docs/evidence/ once the owner's run supplies the real
table and traces, and the outcomes are appended below as a dated
section. These predictions are not edited.
