The six-person validation round, 19 and 20 August 2026
======================================================

Six volunteers, six faces, six devices, one session each, following the
protocol in docs/participant-instructions.md. This is the round the
dry run rehearsed for, and it is the first time any number in this
project was produced by somebody other than the owner. The plan,
including the three failure criteria and every threshold below, was
committed in docs/validation-plan.md before any session file existed,
and nothing in it moved while files were arriving.

The raw CSVs are NOT in this repository and never will be. They sit in
$DATASETS/validation-round. Reproduce this table with:

    cd analysis
    PYTHONPATH="$PWD" .venv/bin/python tools/validation_report.py \
        "$DATASETS/validation-round"

WHAT IS DELIBERATELY NOT PUBLISHED. The participants are pseudonymous
and stay that way: no names, no file names, no export timestamps, no
user agent strings, and no sleepiness self-ratings appear here or
anywhere in this repository. Camera model names are published because
comparing devices is the point of the iris column, and a camera model
is a fact about a product, not a person. Where the text below names a
platform it stops at the class: a Mac in Safari, a Windows laptop in
Chrome, an Android phone, an iPhone.

THE HEADLINE, in one paragraph
------------------------------

The pre-registered baseline criterion FAILED, and the detector
criterion did not. Three of six sessions had no working ruler, each
broken a different way, and the plan's exclusion guard came within one
session of refusing to evaluate the detector at all. Among the three
sessions whose ruler worked, the detector missed nothing; its visible
defect in the wild is the opposite one, over-counting, which appeared
once and matches a defect already on the books. So the component this
round indicts is not the blink detector. It is the baseline learning
that every measurement is judged against.

THE SIX SESSIONS, one paragraph each
------------------------------------

P1, a Mac in Safari at 30 fps. 25 detections for 10 deliberate blinks,
verdict over-counted, on a sound baseline. This person blinks slowly
and deeply, and eleven pairs of detections sit closer together than
0.35 seconds, which an eyelid cannot do twice. The 150 ms refractory
period does not cover their 200 to 270 ms re-crossings. This is the
first evidence from an independent face for the double-counting defect
that previously had only benchmark evidence (issue #178's
neighbourhood), and it feeds the owner's pending decision there.

P2, a Windows laptop in Chrome at 45 fps, and the smallest iris image
this instrument has ever measured, 17.4 pixels, which stayed clean.
Eleven in the window at a metronome 2.15 second cadence, verdict
ambiguous only because the eleventh detection sits 0.46 seconds inside
the marker slack: markers are stamped up to about a second early, so
the table refuses to call a count that a marker shift could change.
Three mark presses instead of two, confirmed accidental.

P3, an Android phone that processed at 24.4 fps, below the 25 fps
floor, for 81 percent of the marked window. Below the floor the
detector is handed nothing, which resets its closure cycle, so this
session's 1 of 10 is not a statement about the detector and the row is
excluded as unsound: its baseline also drifted 34.6 percent, and
uniquely in this round, that drift happened DURING the marked window.
Four mark presses. This session is the round's argument for the
frame-rate soundness rule the next round starts with.

P4, an iPhone at 30 fps. 10 of 10 counted, two mark presses, no tab
switches, the cleanest protocol compliance of the round. Excluded from
criterion 1 anyway, because its baseline drift, 15.4 percent, sits
past the 15 percent ceiling, and the pre-registered rule is the rule.
Stated plainly because the exclusion is marginal: every part of that
drift happened BEFORE the marked window, and the drift inside the
window was 0.0 percent. They caught all ten at 30 fps because they are
a deep blinker, 5.73 mm amplitude on a 7.72 mm baseline, which the
offline sampling experiment predicts is caught at any rate. Do not
read this session as contradicting the processing-rate finding.

P5, a Windows laptop in Chrome with an external webcam, at 40 fps.
Five in the window, verdict missed, excluded as unsound with a
baseline 1.28 times its own median aperture. Both numbers are
contaminated by a protocol order mistake: the long-closure counter
reads 1 at mark 2 and 5 at the session's end, meaning the five second
eye closure was performed INSIDE the ten-blink window, where the
protocol places it after. Nobody blinks with shut eyes, so the
window's biggest hole is behaviour, and five long closures instead of
one drag the session's median aperture down, which is what pushed the
baseline ratio over the line. The closing sleepiness question was
skipped. No double-counting: no gap in this log is under 0.98 seconds.

P6, an iPhone at 46 fps in the window. 10 of 10 counted at a metronome
1.8 to 2.5 second cadence, closure performed correctly after mark 2,
both sleepiness questions answered, no tab switches, and the largest
iris image of the round, 58 pixels. Four mark presses; the first two
bracket the task correctly. The baseline ratio, 1.23, is the round's
narrowest escape: two hundredths under the ceiling, and it decided
that criterion 1 could be evaluated at all. Also a deep blinker,
median amplitude 6.30 mm on an 8.24 mm baseline, so this 10 of 10
cannot separate the rate question from blink depth either.

WHAT THE ROUND ESTABLISHES
--------------------------

1. The baseline does not generalise, and that is now a pre-registered
   FAILED criterion, not an impression. Three of six rulers were
   unusable: one drifted during the measurement itself (P3), one
   drifted past the ceiling before the window (P4), one was born too
   long, inflated by the person's own closures (P5). Every blink
   number this instrument produces is a comparison against that
   ruler. Fixing baseline calibration is the highest-value work this
   round points at.

2. When the ruler works, the detector did not miss. Zero missed among
   the three sound sessions, and the excluded P4 also caught ten of
   ten. The detector's wild-conditions defect is precision:
   25 detections for 10 blinks on one face whose slow deep blinks
   re-cross the line after the 150 ms refractory period expires.

3. The drift-timing pattern supports the next round's window rule.
   Drift measured inside the marked window only: P3 34.65 percent,
   P4 0.0, P5 0.0, P6 0.0. The whole-session drift rule excluded P4
   for movement that happened entirely outside the measurement. The
   pre-registered rule still binds this round, and the next round's
   rule measures drift over the window instead.

4. The frame-rate gate never wrongly opened: every camera declared 30
   and criterion 3 was not met. What the gate lacks is a soundness
   rule ABOVE its own floor: P3 sat at 24.4 fps, the gate fired
   correctly, and the table still needed a human to notice that a
   session the gate had been refusing most of the window could not
   speak about the detector.

5. Protocol compliance is the unmoderated design's real cost. Three
   of six pressed the mark button more than twice, one performed the
   closure inside the blink window, one skipped the closing
   sleepiness question. The revised instructions arrived only after
   the first three sessions, so this round cannot say whether they
   help. The next round should be moderated: a live call while the
   participant runs their own device in their own room, which
   controls the person without faking the environment.

WHAT THE ROUND CANNOT SAY, permanently
--------------------------------------

The blink log is censored: a missed blink writes no row, so every
amplitude in every export comes from blinks that were caught, and no
re-reading of the files can say whether the missed blinks were shallow
ones in the rate band. Both 10-of-10 sessions came from deep blinkers,
so the round adds no clean evidence on either side of the
processing-rate transition, which stays where the dry run and the
offline experiment left it: between 30 and 55 fps on this face, band
closing by 60 Hz offline. And six people is six people: this round is
a smoke test of generality, not a study.

RULES ALREADY QUEUED FOR THE NEXT ROUND, none applied to this one
-----------------------------------------------------------------

1. Frame-rate soundness: a session is not evidence about the detector
   when the gate was refusing frames for a large share of its window.
2. Drift measured over the marked window, not the whole session.
3. A minimum window width, or the window's length printed beside its
   counts: two marks pressed inside the same second currently make a
   zero-width window that prints as an ordinary miss.
4. Moderation, as above.
