APERTURE IS NOT POSE INVARIANT, AND A PREDICTION
================================================

Written 7 September 2026 (roadmap 10.10c4c, ladder B12, audit F-086),
before any number below is computed, in the predict-then-verify
discipline.

WHY IT EXISTS
-------------
Every millimetre this project publishes comes from one expression in
src/core/aperture.ts:

  apertureMm = aperturePx * (IRIS_DIAMETER_MM / irisWidthPx)

The lid opening is measured in pixels and divided by the iris width in
pixels, so the iris is the ruler. That division is what makes the
number independent of how far away the face is, and it is the reason
the figure can be compared across sessions at all.

It does not make the number independent of which way the head is
turned, and the head is allowed to turn. The validity gate refuses a
frame beyond 20 degrees of pitch, 25 of yaw or 25 of roll
(POSE_LIMITS, src/core/constants.ts). Inside those limits every frame
is accepted and every millimetre published as though pose did not
matter.

Pose does matter, and the reason is that the numerator and the
denominator foreshorten on different axes. The lid opening is a
VERTICAL distance; the iris width is a HORIZONTAL one. A nod
foreshortens the first and leaves the second alone. A turn
foreshortens the second and leaves the first alone. So the ratio moves
in opposite directions for the two, and neither cancels.

THE METHOD, FIXED NOW
---------------------
A synthetic eye whose true geometry is known: eyelid and iris ring
landmarks placed on a plane in three dimensions, rotated by a stated
pitch, yaw and roll, projected to the image plane, converted to the
normalised coordinates a landmarker emits, and fed to the REAL
apertureMm with the real index sets. The measured millimetres are
compared against the same eye at zero pose.

The projection is orthographic. A real camera is perspective, and a
perspective term depends on how far the face is from the lens, which
is a parameter this simulation does not have and would have to invent.
Orthographic is the clean floor: whatever perspective adds, it adds on
top of what is measured here. That limitation is stated rather than
hidden, and it is the first place to look if this disagrees with
another measurement.

Roll is rotated in the IMAGE PLANE, in pixels, because that is what a
tilted head does to a camera. Rotating in normalised coordinates
instead would be rotating in a space the pixel conversion stretches
unevenly, which no head does.

THE PREDICTION
--------------
Written before running it. The algebra is stated too, because a
prediction that cannot be checked by hand is a guess with a decimal
point.

1. PITCH foreshortens the lid opening and leaves the ruler alone, so
   the published aperture reads cos(pitch) of the truth. At the gate's
   limit of 20 degrees that is 0.9397, an UNDERSTATEMENT of 6.03
   percent.

2. YAW foreshortens the ruler and leaves the lid opening alone, so the
   published aperture reads 1/cos(yaw) of the truth. At the gate's
   limit of 25 degrees that is 1.1034, an OVERSTATEMENT of 10.34
   percent.

3. ROLL is free. A rotation in the image plane preserves every
   distance in it, so both the numerator and the denominator are
   unchanged and the ratio is exactly invariant. If the simulation
   shows roll costing anything beyond floating-point noise, the
   simulation is rotating in the wrong space.

4. The two that are not free partly CANCEL when they happen together,
   because one is a numerator and the other a denominator: pitch 20
   with yaw 25 gives cos(20)/cos(25) = 1.0368, an overstatement of
   3.68 percent. The worst cases inside the gate are therefore the
   single-axis ones, not the corner.

5. The full span of published aperture inside the accepted pose
   region runs from 6.03 percent low to 10.34 percent high: a spread
   of about 16 percent, on a quantity whose baseline ceiling refuses a
   session for a 25 percent disagreement.

6. The audit's figure for yaw is "up to +12%". This simulation
   predicts +10.34% at the gate's limit and I do not expect to
   reproduce 12 without either a larger angle (12 percent needs 26.8
   degrees, outside the gate) or a perspective term this projection
   does not model. If the measurement lands at 10.3, the audit's 12 is
   recorded as not reproduced rather than quietly adopted.

WHAT THIS ROW DOES NOT DO
-------------------------
It does not divide the chord by cos(pitch), which is the correction
the ladder offers as optional. A correction needs a pose estimate
trustworthy at the frame level, and this row's job is to say how large
the uncorrected error is so that a later row can decide whether
correcting it is worth the dependency.
