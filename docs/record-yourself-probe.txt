THE RECORD-YOURSELF FEASIBILITY PROBE: DOES A BROWSER RECORDING STEP?
====================================================================

Written 13 September 2026, before the probe is run and before any
self-recording is stepped through the pipeline. Roadmap 14.6, whose
Check asks the feasibility probe to PRE-COMMIT its result: a recorded
Blob stepped through the existing pipeline, with an honest refusal for
variable-rate or duration-less recordings by name, which 10.14 now
provides. The two outcomes below are committed now so that whatever the
probe returns, it is scored against a prediction and not rationalised
after the fact.

WHAT THE PROBE ASKS, AND WHY THE ANSWER IS NOT OBVIOUS. Row 14.6 wants a
"record yourself" walkthrough: the person records a short clip of their
own face in the browser and the app measures it, so someone with no
second device can still step a clip through the same pipeline the
Eyeblink8 corpus uses. The feasibility question is narrow and it is the
gate for everything after it: does a clip produced by the browser's own
MediaRecorder survive the existing file path — upload (src/io/videoFile.ts),
calibration and stepping (src/io/videoStepper.ts, src/core/stepCalibration.ts),
the detector — and if it does not, does it FAIL HONESTLY, by a named
refusal, rather than by a fabricated measurement of the kind the
September audit's critical finding (docs/stepper-honesty.txt) was about?

The answer is not obvious because a MediaRecorder recording is not a
prepared corpus clip. Two structural properties of a live browser
recording collide with what the stepper needs, and both are already
visible in the code the probe would run against:

  1. VARIABLE FRAME RATE. A MediaRecorder writes frames at whatever
     cadence its source MediaStream delivers — a webcam under a browser
     stamps each captured frame at its capture instant, not on a fixed
     grid, so the gaps between frame times are not a constant period.
     Row 10.14 (docs/stepper-honesty.txt) made exactly this a first-class
     refusal: src/core/stepCalibration.ts requires every calibration gap
     to be a whole multiple of the smallest within CALIBRATION_QUANTUM_S
     (0.0015 s), and a gap that is no whole multiple is returned as
     `kind: "variableRate"`, naming the first offending gap, before the
     first frame is measured. A live webcam recording is the textbook
     shape that rule refuses.

  2. UNKNOWN DURATION. A Blob written in one MediaRecorder pass often
     carries no duration in its container header, and the browser then
     reports `HTMLVideoElement.duration` as Infinity. videoFile.ts
     already reports this faithfully rather than lying to 0 (its comment,
     lines 94-97: "A clip whose duration the container does not record
     reads Infinity ... the caller decides what an unknown duration means
     to it"). videoStepper.ts's `timelineEnd` then falls back from the
     container duration to the `seekable` range, and to null when neither
     is finite — a clip whose end the browser will not name.

THE PREDICTION, COMMITTED BEFORE THE PROBE RUNS.

  P1 (PRIMARY). A clip recorded from a real webcam through MediaRecorder,
     uploaded and stepped by the existing pipeline unchanged, is REFUSED
     as a variable frame rate by src/core/stepCalibration.ts. This is the
     first gate a clip reaches — calibration runs before any frame is
     measured — so the variable-rate refusal fires before the duration
     question matters. The refusal names the offending gap; the run
     produces no per-second CSV and no blink count, exactly as a corpus
     clip that failed the same rule would.

  P2 (HONESTY, THE POINT OF THE ROW). Whatever the refusal path, the
     outcome is a NAMED refusal or a visible stop, never a fabricated
     rate. If a recording is near-constant enough to pass calibration but
     the schedule then drifts, the inexact-landing tolerance
     (INEXACT_LANDING_TOLERANCE = 0.02) refuses it; if the duration is
     unknown, `timelineEnd` returns null or the seekable end and the run
     stops at a real boundary rather than inventing frames past it. 10.14
     exists precisely so that a clip the instrument cannot measure is
     said to be unmeasurable, and this probe is the first test of that
     guarantee on a clip the browser itself produced.

  P3 (WHAT FEASIBILITY THEN MEANS). Given P1, "record yourself" is NOT
     feasible as a naive drop-in: a raw webcam recording will be refused,
     correctly. The feature the later 14.6 rows can honestly build is
     therefore one of two shapes, and this probe decides which is needed
     rather than assuming: either (a) the recording is constrained to a
     constant rate — a fixed-rate canvas.captureStream at capture, or a
     re-encode to a constant frame rate in preparation, which is a
     heavier step than the lossless stream copy
     analysis/tools/prepare_rldd.py uses for the RLDD corpus (that copy
     preserves the source cadence rather than fixing it, which is why the
     corpus needs 10.14b's coverage cross-check at all) — so that a
     prepared clip, not the raw Blob, reaches the stepper; or (b) the
     walkthrough is scoped to the honest
     refusal itself, showing the person why their self-recording cannot
     be measured and what a measurable one would need. The probe's result
     selects between (a) and (b); it does not pre-judge them.

THE FALSIFIER, FIXED HERE. If a clip recorded from a real webcam through
MediaRecorder steps cleanly — calibration accepts it as constant rate,
inexact_landings is 0, and the pipeline produces a per-second CSV whose
frame count matches the clip's own — then P1 is wrong and one of two
things is true, each with its own consequence:

  - The browser and codec on that platform emitted a constant-rate
    recording (some capture paths do quantise to a fixed grid). Then
    "record yourself" IS feasible directly on that platform, shape (a)'s
    remux is unnecessary there, and the later rows proceed on the raw
    Blob — noting that the guarantee is per-platform, not universal,
    because the next browser may record variable-rate.

  - The recording was in fact variable-rate and the pipeline measured it
    anyway. That is not a feature; it is the 10.14 defect reopened on a
    new input class, and it is fixed — the refusal made to catch it —
    before any record-yourself row ships. A self-recording that steps
    "successfully" without its rate ever being checked is the failure
    this probe exists to rule out.

HOW THE PROBE IS RUN, AND WHAT IT IS NOT. The probe records or
synthesises a MediaRecorder Blob (a canvas.captureStream driven at an
uneven cadence is enough to exercise the variable-rate path without a
camera) and steps it through the real pipeline in a browser, reporting
the calibration verdict, inexact_landings, and the resolved timeline end.
It is a feasibility measurement, not a validation of any blink number: no
accuracy claim is drawn from a self-recording here, because a clip of the
maintainer's own face in unknown lighting carries every limitation the
six-person validation round found in live-camera measurement
(docs/validation-round.txt). The probe answers one question — does the
existing pipeline accept, or honestly refuse, a browser recording — and
the later rows are gated on that answer.
