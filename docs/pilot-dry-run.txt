THE OWNER'S DRY RUN — INCREMENT 11, FIRST ATTEMPT, 31 August 2026

The full pilot protocol, run once end to end by the owner on their
own machine against the deployed page at app commit 7041010, exactly
as docs/assessment-pilot-plan.md prescribes: pseudonym saved, KSS
before, thirty seconds still, mark, ten counted deliberate blinks,
mark, about five seconds eyes closed, about a minute reading, export
CSV, export blink log, stop camera, report shown and exported. The
three raw files stay outside this repository, as every session file
does; this document is what travels.

WHAT THE SESSION SHOWED. Calibration accepted (3543 samples, spread
1.210, ceiling unbound). Camera delivered 30.0 frames per second and
the page read essentially all of them (read fraction 1.002, the
stated unclamped artefact). Pose valid on 100 percent of frames,
zero interruptions, KSS 3 before and after. The ten deliberate
blinks between the two marks were caught ten for ten — the round II
check, on this machine, on this night, found every one. The blink
log carries 47 detections over the 127-second session. The score
printed 63 of 100, with its working: -22 for the eyes-closed share
and -15 for one long closure — both of which are the protocol's own
step 6, five deliberate seconds of closed eyes, being counted
honestly. A moderator debriefing from this report should expect
that shape and say so; the number is the protocol looking at
itself, not the participant's alertness.

THE INSTRUMENT DEFECT, VERBATIM, AND ITS ROOT CAUSE. The researcher
tool, run in pilot mode on the three files, refused the cohort:

  INSTRUMENT DEFECT on P1: the page's exported report does not
  carry the re-derived finding 'WARNED — evidenceRate'.

The defect gate fired on the first real session it ever read, and
it was right. One fact — this session's sampled evidence rate —
appears as THREE numbers: 30.0 in the exported CSV, 30.4 in the
report's conditions section, 29.8 in the report's verdict sentence.
The mechanism: deliveryRates() measures over a rolling five-second
window ending at the moment it is called, and it was called three
times at three moments. The CSV captured mid-run with a full
window. The report was generated seconds after Stop, when the
window had begun draining, and the report's renderer called the
function TWICE — once for the conditions lines, once for the
verdict inputs — milliseconds apart, straddling a sample boundary.
The Python mirror re-derived the verdict from the file's 30.0,
found the page's 29.8 sentence missing, and stopped, as designed.

The root cause is a violation of increment 5's committed rule: the
page may hand the verdict only facts the export carries. The rule
was implemented for the processing-rate fallback (the interpolating
median of the per-record fps column) and missed for sampled_fps,
which still read the live, draining window at render time. A page
that computes one fact freshly at every consumer will eventually
render it three ways; the file is the fact's home, and everything
on the page must say what the file says.

THE FIX, in this increment, guard watched failing first: the
session's delivery rates are measured ONCE — at the first moment
any consumer needs them — and every consumer (the CSV export, the
report's verdict, the report's conditions) reads that one capture,
which resets with the session. A guard now holds main.ts to exactly
one live deliveryRates() call site, so a future consumer cannot
quietly reintroduce a second moment.

ALSO FOUND, cosmetic: the cannot-see block's DROZY sentence
rendered "WACV 2016.." — the citation carries its own full stop and
the generated claim appended another. Fixed in the generator and
regenerated.

WHAT THIS ATTEMPT IS, AND IS NOT. By the plan's own rule, a session
that goes sideways is finished and kept, never quietly redone: this
is dry run one, its files are kept by the owner, and its defect is
recorded here. It is NOT the gate. The gate before any volunteer is
a dry run whose pilot table prints clean — headline, eight
surfaces, report agrees — and that requires a second run on the
fixed build. The protocol steps themselves needed no change, the
report read correctly to its one reader, and the one defect found
was in the instrument, caught by the instrument's own gate, which
is the pilot working.
