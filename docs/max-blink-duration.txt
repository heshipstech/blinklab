Issue #178, reconciled: the duration ceiling does two jobs
==========================================================

18 August 2026. This changes no behaviour and does not move the
constant. That is step 4 of the issue's own suggested order and it is
the owner's.

THE QUESTION. `MAX_BLINK_DURATION_MS` is 500. A Python replay of the
Eyeblink8 corpus on 9 August found that raising it to 1000 recovered 13
detections and moved F1 from 92.1 to 93.6. Closed issue #126 says, in
writing and with a reproduced case, that the same constant is doing
unadvertised work as a noise filter. Both look sound. They point
opposite ways, and the project has carried both beliefs since.

WHY IT COULD NOT BE SETTLED BEFORE. Settling it meant a corpus run or an
argument. The blink-trace harness built on 17 August for the sampling
rate question runs one closure past the real `blinkStep` with every
variable held still except the one under test, so both sides can now be
reproduced in the same framework in milliseconds.

Reproduce with:

    BLINKLAB_PRINT_TABLE=1 npx vitest run test/core/maxBlinkDuration.test.ts

THE ANSWER
==========

The two jobs are real, they do not overlap, and which one the constant
is doing depends entirely on a variable neither side named: WHERE THE
BLINK LINE SITS RELATIVE TO THE RESTING EYELID. That is
`baseline_over_resting`, the fifth check added to the validation round
two days ago for an unrelated reason.

There are three regimes, and the tables above show all three.

1. RATIO BELOW ABOUT 1.9. The blink line sits far under the resting eye.
   Ordinary wobble cannot reach it: a 0.9 mm dip does not even read as
   closed. The ceiling has nothing bad to throw away, so it only throws
   away real long blinks. THE CEILING IS PURE COST HERE, and this is the
   state the eight Eyeblink8 clips of alert people are in, which is why
   the replay saw a gain and no loss.

2. RATIO NEAR 2.0. The blink line has climbed into the resting eye's own
   wobble. Now a dip of 0.9 mm crosses it, arms, reopens, and is counted
   as a blink that never happened. At 200 ms it is counted; at 700 ms
   the ceiling discards it. THE CEILING IS THE ONLY THING STANDING
   BETWEEN THAT NOISE AND THE BLINK COUNT, which is exactly what #126
   said. The band is narrow: at 1.90 nothing is counted, at 1.97 the
   phantom appears.

3. RATIO ABOVE 2.0. The blink line is above the resting eyelid, so the
   eye reads closed at rest and a closure never reopens. Nothing is
   counted at all, real or phantom, and the ceiling is irrelevant again.

So 500 is neither too low nor correct. Both findings are true, of
different sessions.

THE #126 CASE HAS A SECOND DEFENCE NOW, AND IT IS NOT THE CEILING
=================================================================

Worth stating on its own, because it changes what step 4 is choosing
between. #126's reproduced case is a 0.2 mm dip, and on main it is not
counted. #126 credited the ceiling for that. It is not the ceiling.

A 0.2 mm dip never reaches the ARM LINE that fix #114 added afterwards,
so its duration is never consulted at all. A short version of that dip
is refused exactly as flatly as a long one. Removing the hysteresis and
leaving the ceiling in place turns that test red; the ceiling does not
save it.

The ceiling's job is therefore narrower than #126 believed: it defends
only against wobble deep enough to arm, which in the measured optics
above means roughly 0.9 mm rather than 0.2 mm, and only in regime 2.

ONE MUTATION SHOWS BOTH SIDES AT ONCE
=====================================

Setting `MAX_BLINK_DURATION_MS` to 1000, which is precisely what #178
debates, turns exactly two tests red and no others:

    a real blink whose closed phase passes the ceiling is discarded
    a deeper wobble DOES arm, and then only the ceiling stops it

The first is the gain the replay measured. The second is the loss #126
warned about. They are the same edit. Nobody has to choose which
document to believe, because the trade is now visible in one command.

WHAT THE OWNER'S OWN SESSIONS SAY
=================================

Step 4 of the issue requires any decision to be measured against the
owner's recorded geometry as well as the corpus, because the healthy
clips cannot see the #126 failure at all. The five validation dry-run
sessions of 16 and 17 August:

    168 blinks measured
    longest closed phase          333.6 ms   ceiling is 500
    blinks above 400 ms                  0
    baseline over resting          1.12 to 1.41

NEITHER JOB WAS EXERCISED ONCE. No blink came near the ceiling, so it
cost nothing. No session came near ratio 1.9, so it defended nothing.
This geometry cannot distinguish the two positions, and collecting more
of it will not.

WHAT WOULD DECIDE IT
====================

Not more sessions from this face. The regimes are separated by the
baseline ratio, so what is needed is either a session that ratchets past
1.9, or a corpus run of the shipped app rather than the replay. The
issue is explicit that the 13 detections came from a Python replay and
have to be earned by a real run before being quoted, and this note does
not quote them as anything else.

The likely shape of the answer, which the issue already guessed and
these measurements support: the two jobs deserve two constants. The
partition between blink and long closure is one number; a noise guard
that only matters when the baseline has ratcheted is another, and it
would be better expressed as a guard on the baseline ratio than as a
duration. That is a decision, not a finding, and it is not taken here.
