THE IRIS-OCCLUSION SIGNAL, AND A PREDICTION
===========================================

Written 2 September 2026, before any Eyeblink8 iris data is read, in
the predict-then-verify discipline.

WHY IT EXISTS
-------------
The awake corpus autopsy (docs/miss-character.txt) found that about 49
of the 67 Eyeblink8 misses share one behaviour: on a frame a human
marked fully closed, the measured eyelid aperture never dropped
decisively — it hovered near the personal blink line, where a truly
shut eye should read near zero. Two explanations fit that equally, and
the aperture alone cannot separate them:

  (a) the personal blink line is placed too high, so a real dip does
      not clear it — a threshold fix; or
  (b) the aperture landmark itself under-reads a real closure — the
      lid lands but the measured opening does not follow it down, a
      model/landmark limitation no threshold move can rescue.

Telling (a) from (b) needs a SECOND closure measure that does not use
the eyelid landmarks. The iris gives one for free. src/core/aperture.ts
already uses the iris HORIZONTAL diameter (right rim to left rim) as
its millimetre ruler, chosen because the horizontal pair is not
occluded by the lids. The VERTICAL pair (top rim to bottom rim) is the
one the lids cover first as the eye closes. So irisAspectRatio =
vertical chord / horizontal chord is a closure witness read entirely
from the iris rim, independent of the aperture:

  open eye  -> iris is a circle           -> ratio near 1
  closing   -> lid hides the top rim      -> vertical shrinks, ratio falls
  shut      -> iris largely hidden        -> ratio small (IF the model tracks it)

The "IF" is the whole question. MediaPipe's iris refinement fits a
circle, and it may keep predicting a full circle behind a closed lid
rather than tracking the visible rim down. If it does, the ratio does
not fall and the signal is blind to the very closures it was meant to
catch.

WHAT THE ONE PIECE OF REAL DATA SO FAR SAYS
-------------------------------------------
The recorded fixture test/fixtures (session01, the owner's own mostly
open-eyed calibration clip, 300 frames, two real blinks) reads, over
both irises:

  open-eye iris aspect ratio: median 0.92, p10 0.89, range 0.83 to 1.08

Two things stand out. The open-eye baseline is 0.92, not 1.0 — the
model already reports a slightly vertically-compressed iris on an open
eye, so the signal starts with less room to fall than a perfect circle
would give. And the fixture's two DETECTED blinks (the aperture
crossed the threshold on them) pulled the ratio no lower than about
0.83 — barely below the open baseline. That is a small sample from one
well-lit clip, not the Eyeblink8 misses, but it leans toward (b)/the
model hallucinating the circle.

THE PREDICTION
--------------
Scored on a corpus run that captures irisAspectRatio in the per-frame
trace, over the ~49 shallow-aperture misses (the above_line misses plus
the never_armed crossed_line misses of
docs/evidence/2026-09-02-awake-autopsy/crossed_line_gate_attribution.csv):

  The iris ratio does NOT collapse. Its median over the closed spans of
  those misses stays above 0.80 — within reach of the 0.92 open
  baseline, nowhere near the ~0.5 a tracked full occlusion would give.
  The model holds a near-circular iris behind the closing lid, so the
  iris signal does not independently see these closures.

If that holds, the ~49 misses are not a threshold bug and not
rescuable by fusing a second landmark signal: the closure is invisible
to this face model, a recall CEILING, and the honest next move is to
say so plainly rather than to move a line. It also retires the second
half of route A.

WHAT EACH OTHER OUTCOME REDIRECTS
---------------------------------
  If the ratio DOES collapse on the misses (median below ~0.70 while
  the aperture stayed near its line), then the aperture landmark
  under-reads a real closure the iris still sees: the fix is to fuse or
  cross-check the two signals, and it would be gated on its own
  predict-then-verify before any counted number changes.

  If the ratio collapses AND the aperture also dips on re-inspection,
  the miss was a threshold-margin case after all (route (a)), and the
  line, not the landmark, is what to reconsider.

This prediction is not edited after the trace is read; the outcome
becomes a new dated section below it.


THE IRIS RESULT, 2 September 2026
================================

The awake corpus run captured irisAspectRatio in the per-frame trace,
and the neutrality check held: the same 67 misses, digit for digit.
The iris ratio was read inside each of the 49 shallow-aperture misses'
closed spans and against that clip's own open baseline in the 60
frames before the span. Per-miss table:
docs/evidence/2026-09-02-awake-autopsy/iris_on_misses_summary.csv.

The prediction is CONFIRMED. The iris does not collapse:

  in-span iris median over the 49 misses:      0.846
  open baseline just before them (median):      0.877
  drop, baseline to in-span (median):          +0.024
  misses with an in-span median below 0.80:     1 of 49
  misses with any in-span frame below 0.70:     0 of 49
  above_line 0.845, never_armed 0.858 (same)

The iris aspect ratio is essentially FLAT from the open eye into the
closed span — a 0.024 drop, nowhere near the collapse toward ~0.5 that
a tracked occlusion would give. On the very frames a human marked
fully shut, the model holds a near-circular iris behind the lid, just
as the pessimistic prediction feared. Both landmark-derived closure
signals are blind here: the aperture hovered near its line (that is
what put these misses in the shallow-aperture set) AND the iris held
its open shape.

WHAT THIS SETTLES. The ~49 shallow-aperture misses are a recall
CEILING, not a tunable defect. The closure is invisible to this face
model's landmarks, so neither moving the blink line (a threshold fix)
nor fusing the iris signal (this experiment) can recover them: there
is no signal to threshold or to fuse. Route A is closed. Lifting this
part of the recall deficit needs a face model that registers these
closures, or ground truth that is not marking closures too subtle for
the model to see; it is not reachable from this repository's
parameters.

CAVEAT, kept honest. This shows the iris does not FALL on the misses;
a stronger form would add a positive control — the iris on closures
the detector DID catch — to confirm the signal has any dynamic range
at all. The recorded fixture's two detected blinks (0.83, against a
0.92 open) already suggest the range is small, and the flat
open-to-closed drop here says the same, but the control is the honest
next check before calling the iris signal useless in general rather
than useless HERE.

WHAT REMAINS TUNABLE. Of the 67 misses, ~49 are this ceiling. The
only recall left that parameters can move is the ~5 re-arm/refractory
state-machine drops (docs/miss-character.txt, a real but small
route B) and, as a design decision rather than a bug, the 9
long-closure misses the 500 ms max-blink-duration rejects.


THE POSITIVE CONTROL, PRE-REGISTERED, 5 September 2026 (roadmap 10.5)
=====================================================================

Written before any caught-blink iris data is read, to close the caveat
the section above left standing: the iris result shows the ratio does
not FALL on the misses, but not whether it falls on anything. The
control reads the same signal on the blinks the detector DID catch —
the 341 matched found blinks of the awake corpus run — against each
clip's own open baseline just before each span.

THE EXTRACTION CONTRACT. Same shape as the miss extraction: per-frame
rows with columns clip, blink_id, spanStart, spanEnd, frameIndex,
irisAspectRatio, insideSpan (apertureMm and blinkLineMm may ride along
and are not read). Spans are the detector's own found-blink frame
spans; the baseline is up to 60 frames before spanStart with
insideSpan 0. The extraction is produced on the machine that holds the
measured folder, from the tools/measure_corpus.mjs per-frame traces —
the raw traces stay uncommitted as before; only the per-blink table of
numbers lands under docs/evidence/. The committed tool,
analysis/tools/iris_positive_control.py, computes the per-blink table
(open_baseline_iris_median, in_span_iris_median, in_span_iris_min per
blink) and prints the caught-blink numbers beside the committed miss
numbers.

THE PREDICTION. The iris does not show usable dynamic range on the
caught blinks either:

  (i)  the median across caught blinks of the per-blink in-span
       MINIMUM stays at or above 0.75, and
  (ii) fewer than 25% of caught blinks show any in-span frame
       below 0.70.

Grounds: the recorded fixture's two detected blinks bottomed at ~0.83
against a 0.92 open baseline; the model held a near-circular iris
behind lids a human marked fully shut; MediaPipe's iris refinement
fits a circle, and that mechanism has no reason to spare deeper
closures at ~30 fps, where the fully shut moment spans a frame or two.

THE DECISION RULE, fixed now. Over analyzable caught blinks (at least
20 required, else the tool refuses; a blink with no finite in-span
ratio or fewer than 10 finite baseline frames is dropped and counted,
and more than 20% dropped is a refusal):

  range_absent   fewer than 25% of blinks dip below 0.70
  range_present  75% or more of blinks dip below 0.70
  partial        anything between, reported as measured, both
                 readings stated, no simple sentence claimed

WHAT EACH OUTCOME MEANS. range_absent: the iris signal is useless as a
closure witness at these optics IN GENERAL, and the caveat closes in
its stronger form — not merely useless on the misses. range_present:
the signal has range, so the 49 misses' flatness is specific to
closures the model cannot see — the ceiling conclusion sharpens rather
than weakens, and fusing the iris for BORDERLINE cases becomes a
candidate again, gated on its own predict-then-verify. partial: the
per-blink table is the result.

This prediction is not edited after the extraction is read; the
outcome becomes a new dated section below.
